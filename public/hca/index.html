Asheville Watchdog / NC Health News October 3, 2026 Lawsuit

Private Antitrust Suit Against HCA/Mission Eyes Settlement — But Class Certification Denied

A North Carolina judge denied class-action certification in a private antitrust lawsuit accusing HCA and Mission of using their regional monopoly to force insurers into anticompetitive contracts — a significant procedural setback for plaintiffs. Separately, the Asheville Watchdog reports HCA, Mission, and the plaintiffs have reached an agreement in principle to settle, which could end the yearslong legal fight. The outcome does not affect the NC Attorney General's separate lawsuit, which survived summary judgment on July 28, 2026.

How to position this
  • A possible settlement of the private antitrust case does not resolve the NC AG lawsuit — that case survived summary judgment on July 28 and continues to move forward.
  • The class certification denial shows how difficult it is for individual patients and premium-payers to get justice in court — strengthening the case for AG-level and legislative accountability.
  • The underlying monopoly allegation — that HCA/Mission used 58%+ regional market share to coerce anticompetitive insurer contracts — is still at the core of the AG's claims.
  • Any settlement should be evaluated against Mission's documented harms: 4 Immediate Jeopardy citations, a staffing collapse from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE/bed, and the federal monitor's July 2026 noncompliance finding.

On October 3, 2026, NC Health News (via Asheville Watchdog) reported that a state judge denied class certification in the private antitrust case against HCA and Mission Hospital, finding the plaintiffs' methodology insufficient to show real-world premium impacts on class members. The same day, the Asheville Watchdog reported that HCA, Mission, and plaintiffs reached an agreement in principle to settle. The private antitrust suit alleged HCA and Mission leveraged their Asheville-area monopoly to coerce insurers into anticompetitive contracts. The NC Attorney General's separate AG lawsuit — which survived a July 28, 2026 summary judgment motion — continues on a separate track and is not affected by this settlement.

Asheville Watchdog / GovInfo September 8, 2026 Congress

Rep. Edwards Introduces 'HCA Mission Act' to Create Federal Penalty for Immediate Jeopardy Violations

Rep. Chuck Edwards (R-NC-11) introduced H.R. 10311, the Health Care Accountability Mission Act of 2026, on September 8, 2026. The bill would amend the Social Security Act to establish a new civil monetary penalty specifically for hospitals that fail to comply with Medicare participation requirements in ways that immediately jeopardize patient health or safety — directly targeting the type of violations Mission Hospital has faced. The bill was referred to the House Committee on Ways and Means.

How to position this
  • Mission Hospital has now received 4 Immediate Jeopardy citations under HCA's ownership (2021, 2024, 2025, 2026) — the exact pattern H.R. 10311 would penalize with new civil monetary fines.
  • The bill was referred to Ways and Means — the same committee where HCA CEO Sam Hazen testified in April 2026 and said nothing about Mission's IJ violations, staffing collapse, or federal noncompliance.
  • Current law allows CMS to threaten Medicare/Medicaid termination for IJ violations but lacks a targeted monetary penalty; this bill would close that gap and give regulators a more calibrated enforcement tool.
  • Advocates should urge Ways and Means to hold hearings and press HCA executives on Mission's four IJ citations before the committee takes up any hospital deregulation (e.g., CON elimination) proposals Hazen endorsed.

H.R. 10311, introduced by Rep. Chuck Edwards (R-NC-11) on September 8, 2026, would amend Title XVIII of the Social Security Act to add civil monetary penalties for hospitals whose noncompliance rises to the level of immediate jeopardy — the same CMS designation Mission Hospital has received four times since HCA's 2019 acquisition. The bill is referred to the House Committee on Ways and Means — the same committee before which HCA CEO Sam Hazen testified in April 2026 without addressing Mission's IJ citations or federal noncompliance. Mission is the nation's most prominent example of a repeat Immediate Jeopardy hospital still operating under the same ownership; the bill implicitly targets this pattern.

Nashville Banner / WKRN September 2, 2026 Safety

HCA Cuts 200+ Corporate Jobs in Nashville, Citing Rising Costs and Payer-Mix Losses

HCA Healthcare confirmed in early September 2026 that it laid off more than 200 employees from its Nashville information technology and corporate operations, citing rising costs, increases in uninsured patients, and shifts in healthcare coverage mix. The cuts come as HCA slashed its full-year 2026 EPS guidance and as Mission Hospital in Asheville continues to operate with federal noncompliance findings and no submitted staffing plan. Critics note that corporate cost-cutting at HCA's Nashville headquarters mirrors the staffing reductions already imposed on frontline workers at Mission.

How to position this
  • HCA cuts corporate jobs in Nashville to protect margins while Mission Hospital's bedside staffing ratio sits at 3.7 FTE/bed — well below the NC average of 5.1 — with no staffing plan submitted to the federal monitor.
  • The layoffs are a direct consequence of HCA's guidance cut driven by payer-mix losses and ACA enrollment changes — the same financial pressures executives use to justify not investing in Mission staffing.
  • HCA's pattern of cost-cutting at every level (Nashville IT, Mission bedside nursing) while rewarding shareholders with buybacks and dividends raises urgent questions about where patient safety ranks in the corporate priority stack.
  • Congress should ask HCA: if the company can afford a $10 billion share buyback authorization, why are 200+ corporate workers being cut and why has Mission submitted no staffing plan to federal monitors?

HCA Healthcare confirmed in early September 2026 that it was reducing a 'small percentage' of positions — more than 200 jobs — at its Nashville corporate and IT operations, citing rising costs and unfavorable payer mix shifts including increasing numbers of uninsured patients. The layoffs follow HCA's July 2026 downward revision of full-year EPS guidance (to $28.70–$30.50) and the company's acknowledgment of ACA enrollment losses. For Mission Hospital workers and patients in Asheville, this corporate belt-tightening reflects the same profit-over-people logic that drove frontline staffing down from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE per bed — while a federal monitor reported in July 2026 that Mission had submitted no staffing improvement plans.

Washington Post / KFF Health News August 9, 2026 Safety

National Investigation: Mission Hospital's Monopoly Pricing — Knee Replacement $40K vs. $16K Elsewhere

A major Washington Post/KFF Health News investigation by Peter Whoriskey exposed Mission Hospital's monopoly-driven pricing in Asheville: under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement cost roughly $40,000 at Mission versus about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center. A 2024 RAND study placed Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare — far above the 280% NC benchmark and Catawba's 237%. The piece traces the pricing power to Mission's 1998 merger-built monopoly, the 2015 repeal of NC profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of what one economist called a 'prepackaged monopoly.'

How to position this
  • Mission's monopoly pricing — 334% of Medicare vs. a 280% NC benchmark — is a direct tax on every WNC employer, family, and insured patient, enabled by HCA's unchallenged market dominance.
  • The same insurer pays nearly 2.5× more for a knee replacement at Mission than at a comparable NC hospital; this gap compounds HCA's 2-star HCAHPS patient experience scores — high prices, poor outcomes.
  • HCA CEO Sam Hazen testified before Ways and Means in April 2026 on hospital affordability without addressing Mission's above-benchmark commercial pricing or the ongoing federal noncompliance finding.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Mayfield's inclusion in the KFF story validates the community's accountability work — use this piece as a primary reference when educating legislators and media on HCA's WNC monopoly.

Investigative reporter Peter Whoriskey (Washington Post / KFF Health News) documented a stark price gap driven by Mission Hospital's regional monopoly: knee replacements run ~$40,000 at Mission vs. ~$16,000 at Catawba Valley under the same Blue Cross plan; breast biopsies $7,500 vs. $1,700 (UnitedHealthcare); hernia repair $17,700 vs. $9,600. A 2024 RAND study pegged Mission's commercial rates at 334% of Medicare versus a 280% NC benchmark. The story features employer Katie Button, patient Marcelle Crago, Yale economist Zack Cooper, and Sen. Julie Mayfield of Reclaim Healthcare WNC. It traces the pricing power to Mission's 1998 consolidation, the 2015 repeal of NC profit limits, and HCA's 2019 acquisition of the resulting monopoly.

NC DOJ / Blue Ridge Public Radio July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA Summary Judgment — NC AG Lawsuit Proceeds Toward Trial

A North Carolina judge denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, 2026, allowing NC Attorney General Jeff Jackson's lawsuit to advance. The AG alleges HCA violated the legally binding commitments made when it purchased Mission Hospital in 2019 — including service, staffing, and access obligations. The denial means the court found sufficient disputed facts for trial, and the case now moves forward, backed by the July 2026 federal monitor finding that Mission remains out of substantial compliance.

How to position this
  • The court's refusal to dismiss the AG lawsuit on summary judgment means HCA could not convince a judge that its conduct was lawful as a matter of law — the case now proceeds with full legal scrutiny.
  • The federal monitor's concurrent July 2026 noncompliance finding — no staffing plans submitted, potential service-discontinuation violations — corroborates the AG's core claims.
  • Mission has received 4 Immediate Jeopardy citations since HCA's acquisition; the AG lawsuit is the primary accountability mechanism ensuring those violations carry legal consequences.
  • Supporters should contact AG Jackson's office to express support and urge the legislature to fund the litigation through to a final judgment or enforceable settlement with real benchmarks.

On July 28, 2026, a North Carolina judge denied HCA Healthcare's motion for summary judgment in the AG's lawsuit alleging violations of the 2019 Mission Hospital acquisition commitments. The ruling means the court found enough disputed material facts to proceed — a significant milestone for accountability advocates. AG Jeff Jackson filed the lawsuit arguing HCA failed to honor enforceable commitments on staffing, services, and patient access. The federal monitor simultaneously reported in July 2026 that Mission is not in substantial compliance with its acquisition obligations and has submitted no staffing improvement plans.
