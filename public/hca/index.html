KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

Same Surgery, Twice the Price: KFF Investigation Exposes Mission Hospital's Monopoly Premium

A major investigation by KFF Health News and the Washington Post finds that the same knee-replacement procedure cost roughly $40,000 at Mission Hospital under one Blue Cross plan — more than double the ~$16,000 charged at Catawba Valley Medical Center. RAND data shows Mission prices at 334% of Medicare, versus a 280% NC benchmark and 237% at Catawba. Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC are featured as voices calling for accountability.

How to position this
  • Mission charges more than double the regional rate for common procedures — a direct result of the monopoly HCA purchased in 2019.
  • RAND data: Mission prices are 334% of Medicare, well above the 280% NC average and 237% at comparable NC hospitals.
  • HCA's monopoly grip — covering 58% of Asheville-area discharges — lets it raise prices without competitive check while staffing collapses.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Mayfield are on the record: Asheville is a cautionary tale for the nation on unregulated hospital monopolies.

Investigative reporter Peter Whoriskey documents how Mission Hospital's 1998 monopoly merger, a 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of what Yale economist Zack Cooper called a 'prepackaged monopoly' have driven prices far above state and national norms. Under the same UnitedHealthcare plan, a breast biopsy ran $7,500 at Mission versus $1,700 elsewhere and a hernia repair cost $17,700 versus $9,600. Patient Marcelle Crago and restaurant owner Katie Button illustrate how WNC residents bear the burden. Sen. Mayfield warns that 'unregulated monopolies have never gone well for the public.'

Independent Monitor: HCA Contractually Barred from Selling Mission for 10 Years; Noncompliance Concerns Remain

Members of the independent monitoring team took public questions on August 4 about HCA's 2019 acquisition of Mission Hospital. Monitor representative Rachel Ryan confirmed that under the Asset Purchase Agreement, HCA cannot sell Mission without advisory board consent during the first 10 years, and said the monitor is unaware of any HCA intent to sell. The 2025 monitor report still identifies two areas of potential noncompliance: service discontinuation and repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations.

How to position this

  • Two areas of potential APA noncompliance persist: service cuts and four Immediate Jeopardy citations since 2019.
  • HCA has repeatedly delayed and limited data production to the independent monitor, hampering meaningful oversight.
  • Community engagement is growing — 90+ residents attended the webinar, showing WNC residents are watching HCA's every move.

The Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors hosted a community Q&A webinar on August 4 drawing 90-plus attendees, followed by WLOS coverage on August 5. Rachel Ryan of Dogwood Health Trust clarified that the APA obligates HCA not to sell Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board consent and that the monitor is unaware of any sale desire. Ryan restated the 2025 report's two areas of potential noncompliance: discontinued industrial rehabilitation services and the pattern of Immediate Jeopardy citations in 2025 and 2026. The monitor noted ongoing limited cooperation and data delays from HCA.

NC DOJ / Blue Ridge Public Radio July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA's Motion for Summary Judgment — AG Jackson's Mission Hospital Lawsuit Proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp denied HCA Healthcare's motion for summary judgment on July 28, ruling in favor of several of AG Jeff Jackson's arguments in a 55-page order. AG Jackson said HCA 'threw every argument against the wall to try to get this case thrown out, but they didn't stick.' The lawsuit alleging HCA violated its acquisition commitments on emergency care, oncology, and staffing now moves forward.

How to position this
  • A federal judge rejected HCA's attempt to end the AG lawsuit — the case is proceeding on HCA's broken promises to WNC communities.
  • Key claims on oncology access and service cuts survived summary judgment, giving advocates powerful judicial validation.
  • CMS has found Mission in Immediate Jeopardy four times since HCA's 2019 acquisition — the AG's office cited this record directly in the ruling context.
  • HCA's legal defense strategy failed; the court saw through arguments that the case lacked merit.

In a 55-page ruling, Judge Earp rejected HCA's bid to end the AG's lawsuit before it could go further, upholding key arguments about HCA's obligations under the Asset Purchase Agreement, including on oncology and service access. AG Jackson's office noted that CMS has found Mission in Immediate Jeopardy four times since 2019, most recently in January 2026 — the most serious deficiency category, meaning patient safety was placed at risk. HCA has maintained the allegations are 'wholly without merit.' The case now advances toward trial, keeping HCA's Mission-related commitments under active judicial scrutiny.

National Nurses United / Too Big to Care Evergreen reference Safety

NNU Case Study: HCA's 'Anticompetitive Model' — Asheville a Central Chapter

National Nurses United's Too Big to Care case study documents HCA's national playbook of market domination, price increases, service cuts, and aggressive billing — with Mission Hospital a central example. Key findings include: Mission covers 58% of Asheville-area discharges; 15 trauma nurses left in 2020; HCA introduced bedside collections and trauma activation fees up to $50,000; and Mission nurses won a landmark union vote in September 2020 — the largest non-union Southern hospital organizing win since 1975. The study situates Mission's crisis within HCA's deliberate corporate strategy.

How to position this
  • Mission's crisis is not an accident — NNU documents HCA's national playbook of market domination, service cuts, and price extraction.
  • HCA controls 58% of Asheville-area discharges, enabling unchecked pricing and quality cuts with no competitive alternative.
  • Mission nurses organized in 2020 — the largest non-union Southern hospital union win since 1975 — as a direct response to HCA's ownership practices.
  • Trauma activation fees up to $50,000 and bedside collections are part of HCA's documented aggressive billing strategy at Mission.

The NNU Too Big to Care case study, 'HCA: The Vanguard of the Anticompetitive Model,' shows that Mission Hospital's staffing collapse, price hikes, and service cuts are not isolated failures — they follow HCA's documented national blueprint. The report notes approximately 79 doctors departed Mission, surprise bills appeared post-2019, bedside collections were introduced, and class-action antitrust suits were filed by Asheville-area patients. Mission nurses' historic 2020 union vote is framed as a direct response to HCA's ownership. The case study is an essential reference for advocates connecting local harm to national corporate strategy.

2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
Hazen testimony

Jul 28, 2026
