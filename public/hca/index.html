KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly Pricing

Same Knee Replacement, Twice the Price: National Investigation Names Mission as Monopoly Pricing Case Study

A major Washington Post / KFF Health News investigation documents how HCA's Mission Hospital charges dramatically more than regional competitors — roughly $40,000 for a knee replacement versus $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center. The piece traces the monopoly back to Mission's 1998 merger and HCA's 2019 purchase of what one economist called a 'prepackaged monopoly.' Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC are cited as accountability voices.

How to position this
  • Mission charges up to 2.5× regional competitors for the same procedures under the same insurance plan — a direct result of the monopoly HCA inherited and has exploited.
  • RAND data places Mission at 334% of Medicare rates, 54 points above the NC benchmark — some of the highest commercial pricing in the state.
  • HCA responded to a patient complaint by saying charges 'represent the cost for supporting the entire episode of care' — while staffing ratios have fallen from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE per bed.
  • State profit-limit repeal in 2015 and the 2019 HCA acquisition combined to turn a nonprofit community asset into a for-profit pricing machine; this investigation gives advocates a national platform to demand accountability.

Reporter Peter Whoriskey found that under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement at Mission cost about $40,000 compared to roughly $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center. A breast biopsy ran $7,500 at Mission versus $1,700 elsewhere; a hernia repair was $17,700 versus $9,600. RAND 2024 data shows Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare, well above the 237% at Catawba and the NC benchmark of 280%. Yale economist Zack Cooper and patient advocate Marcelle Crago are among those featured, alongside Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC.

WLOS August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent Monitor: HCA Cannot Sell Mission for 10 Years — and Potential Noncompliance Stands

Following the August 4 community Q&A webinar, WLOS reported on monitor representative Rachel Ryan's clarification that HCA is contractually barred from selling Mission Hospital for the first 10 years of the Asset Purchase Agreement without advisory board consent. Ryan also restated the 2025 report's finding of potential noncompliance tied to service discontinuations and multiple Immediate Jeopardy designations.

How to position this
  • HCA cannot exit Mission for a decade — western NC communities are locked in with an operator the federal monitor says is not in substantial compliance.
  • Potential noncompliance on service discontinuation means patients may be losing access to care HCA contractually promised to maintain.
  • Recurring Immediate Jeopardy designations (4 since 2019) are explicitly cited by the monitor as grounds for potential noncompliance — a fact HCA's public communications continue to minimize.
  • The community Q&A format, while welcomed, underscores how little routine transparency HCA provides; advocates should press for binding reporting requirements.

Members of the independent monitoring team took public questions on August 4 following the release of the 2025 compliance report. When asked about the possibility of another health system such as UNC Health taking over Mission, monitor rep Rachel Ryan said she was unaware of any HCA desire to sell but clarified that under the APA, HCA is obligated not to sell Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board consent. Ryan also reaffirmed that the 2025 report identified potential noncompliance related to service discontinuation and Mission's recurring Immediate Jeopardy citations.

Judge Denies HCA's Summary Judgment Motion — AG Jackson's Lawsuit Against HCA Proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp ruled in favor of several of AG Jeff Jackson's arguments and largely denied HCA's motion for summary judgment, allowing the lawsuit over Mission Hospital's operation to proceed. The ruling means HCA must defend its conduct in court rather than escape accountability before trial.

  • The simultaneous noncompliance notification from Dogwood to the AG gives the state additional ammunition as the lawsuit advances.
  • Advocates should keep calling this a 'lawsuit' proceeding toward trial — not a resolved matter — and use the July 28 ruling to demand legislative hearings on HCA accountability.

On July 28, 2026, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp largely denied HCA Healthcare's motion for summary judgment, ruling in favor of several arguments advanced by Attorney General Jeff Jackson. The lawsuit — focused on HCA's alleged violations of commitments made when it acquired Mission Hospital in 2019 — will proceed. Acquisition
