NC Health News / Asheville Watchdog August 25, 2026 CON

92 New WNC Hospital Beds Up for Grabs Amid Community Anger at HCA

More than 100 people packed a public CON hearing at A-B Tech as Mission, Novant, UNC Health, and AdventHealth competed for 92 additional acute-care beds in Buncombe County. Community members voiced deep anger at HCA and distrust of the regulatory process that already granted Mission 95 beds in March — despite four Immediate Jeopardy citations. State Sen. Julie Mayfield said she was 'shocked and puzzled' by the earlier award.

How to position this
  • DHHS awarded Mission 95 CON beds in March while the hospital was under active federal safety sanctions — a decision Sen. Mayfield called 'shocking and puzzling.'
  • Mission's expansion bid comes even as it has never submitted court-ordered staffing plans and the federal monitor confirmed noncompliance in July 2026.
  • Community anger at the CON hearing reflects years of HCA-driven staffing collapse — FTE/bed ratios fell from 6.1 to 3.7 vs. a 5.1 NC average.
  • Advocates should demand regulators apply safety performance conditions to any future Mission CON awards, not grant beds as a reward for a hospital under federal sanction.

A packed public hearing at Asheville-Buncombe Technical College's Ferguson Auditorium saw four health systems pitch for 92 additional hospital beds in Buncombe County. Mission Hospital — already licensed for 853 beds after a March CON award of 95 beds — is competing alongside Novant, UNC Health West, and AdventHealth. The March decision to award beds to Mission despite four federal Immediate Jeopardy citations since 2021 drew sharp criticism from community advocates and legislators. UNCH and AdventHealth are appealing the March decision.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly Pricing

Investigation: Mission Hospital Charges More Than Double for Same Surgery Under Same Insurance Plan

A major KFF Health News/Washington Post investigation documents how HCA's monopoly over western NC drives sky-high prices: a knee replacement at Mission ran roughly $40,000 under a Blue Cross plan versus about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center. Rand 2024 data pegs Mission commercial prices at 334% of Medicare — far above the 280% NC benchmark and the 237% at Catawba. Yale economist Zack Cooper and Sen. Julie Mayfield warned that unregulated hospital monopolies never end well for the public.

How to position this
  • Mission charges 334% of Medicare for commercial patients — 54 points above the NC benchmark and nearly 100 points above comparable Catawba Valley Medical Center.
  • Under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement cost ~$40,000 at HCA's Mission vs. ~$16,000 at a competing NC hospital — more than double.
  • HCA bought Mission as a 'prepackaged monopoly' in 2019, then raised prices while cutting staff; the investigation directly connects market dominance to price gouging.
  • Every employer and premium-payer in western NC subsidizes HCA's monopoly margins — Asheville is a national cautionary tale for communities facing hospital consolidation.

Peter Whoriskey's investigation for KFF Health News and the Washington Post traces how Mission Hospital's monopoly — rooted in a 1998 merger and supercharged by HCA's 2019 purchase of a 'prepackaged monopoly' — produces commercial prices at 334% of Medicare, against a 237% Catawba Valley benchmark and a 280% NC average. The piece features patients facing $40,000 knee replacements, $7,500 breast biopsies, and $17,700 hernia repairs versus far lower costs at competing facilities. Employer Katie Button and Yale's Zack Cooper explain how monopoly pricing ripples into every premium-payer's wallet. Sen. Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC are cited as advocates warning other communities.

WLOS August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent Monitor: HCA Cannot Sell Mission for First 10 Years Without Advisory Board Consent

Following the August 4 community webinar, WLOS reported that monitor representative Rachel Ryan confirmed HCA is contractually prohibited from selling Mission during the first 10 years of the Asset Purchase Agreement without advisory board consent — and that the monitor is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. Ryan also restated the 2025 report's findings of potential noncompliance on service discontinuation and multiple Immediate Jeopardy designations.

How to position this
  • HCA is contractually barred from selling Mission for 10 years without advisory board consent — a critical protection advocates should track if financial pressure mounts.
  • The monitor confirmed two areas of potential APA noncompliance: service cuts and repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations — both now before the NC Attorney General.
  • Repeated IJ citations are themselves a contractual compliance issue under the APA, not just a CMS problem — the monitoring process directly feeds the AG lawsuit.
  • Community members deserve transparent, regular updates from the monitor; the August 4 webinar (90+ attendees) shows strong public demand for accountability.

WLOS covered the independent monitor's public Q&A on HCA's ownership of Mission Hospital following release of the 2025 monitoring report. Monitor representative Rachel Ryan clarified that under the APA, HCA is obligated not to sell Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board consent, and that she is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. Ryan restated the report's two areas of potential noncompliance: discontinuation of certain services and repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations. Over 90 community members attended the August 4 webinar hosted by Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors.

NC Department of Justice July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

AG Jackson Wins Key Arguments: HCA/Mission Lawsuit Proceeds After Judge Denies Summary Judgment

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, 2026, allowing AG Jeff Jackson's lawsuit to advance. The AG alleges HCA failed to provide the emergency and cancer care it committed to when it purchased Mission Health in 2019. Jackson said he will 'keep fighting to defend emergency and cancer care in western North Carolina.' CMS has cited Mission for Immediate Jeopardy four times since the 2019 acquisition.

How to position this
  • A judge found sufficient evidence for the AG's claims to go forward — HCA cannot dismiss allegations of broken acquisition promises with a motion.
  • The AG lawsuit directly targets HCA's core claim that it has honored its Mission commitments on emergency and cancer care — the court disagreed.
  • Four Immediate Jeopardy citations since 2019, confirmed by the judge's ruling context, are central evidence that HCA's promised care has not materialized.
  • Community members and advocates should know: this is not over — the lawsuit proceeds and HCA must now defend its record in court.

North Carolina Attorney General Jeff Jackson's lawsuit against HCA Healthcare cleared a major legal hurdle on July 28, 2026, when NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment. The case centers on allegations that HCA failed to fulfill commitments on emergency and cancer care made at the time of its $1.5 billion acquisition of Mission Health in 2019. The AG's office noted that CMS has issued four Immediate Jeopardy citations to Mission since the sale. The lawsuit now proceeds toward trial.

HCA Slashes 2026 Earnings Guidance as ACA Coverage Losses Create $1B+ Headwind

HCA Healthcare cut its full-year 2026 net income guidance to $6.3–$6.7 billion (from $6.5–$7.0 billion) after ACA exchange disruptions shifted more patients to uninsured status, creating an estimated $1.0–$1.2 billion adjusted EBITDA headwind. HCA Healthcare lowered its 2026 full-year net income guidance on July 14, citing a $1.0–$1.2 billion adjusted EBITDA headwind from declining ACA exchange enrollment, as patients losing subsidized coverage converted to uninsured status at higher-than-expected rates. Arc — running narrative

2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
Hazen testimony

Jul 26, 2026
Deadline passes
The Enhanced Plan of Correction deadline expires. The federal monitor confirms Mission has not achieved substantial compliance and has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the same deficiencies driving every prior sanction.
Jul 28, 2026

Judge denies HCA's summary judgment motion. The NC Attorney General's lawsuit will go to trial. A court will now assess whether HCA honored its legal commitments to WNC. Reclaim's years of documentation, testimony, and public pressure have contributed directly to this moment.
