Asheville Watchdog August 25, 2026 Safety

Wrongful-Death Suit Filed: Nurse Failed to Act for 51 Minutes During Routine Foot Surgery

A wrongful-death lawsuit filed August 21 in Buncombe County Superior Court alleges that a Mission Hospital nurse anesthetist watched a 64-year-old patient's vitals deteriorate for 51 minutes without intervening during a routine foot surgery, leading to cardiac arrest and death. The estate of Noe Serrano Hernandez targets HCA Healthcare directly. The case is the latest in a string of patient-harm allegations tied to Mission's chronic staffing and oversight failures under HCA ownership.

How to position this
  • This wrongful-death case fits a pattern: CMS found systemic 'failure to rescue' deficiencies at Mission in multiple consecutive inspection cycles.
  • Mission's nurse staffing ratio fell from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE per bed under HCA — half-staffed units increase the risk that no one acts when a patient crashes.
  • HCA's earnings call reported $2.1 billion in share buybacks in Q2 2026 alone — capital returned to investors while Mission lacked adequate bedside oversight.
  • Advocates should connect individual cases like Serrano Hernandez's to the broader pattern CMS labeled 'systemic and recurring.'

The estate of Noe Serrano Hernandez filed a wrongful-death suit August 21, 2026, alleging a Mission nurse anesthetist failed to respond to deteriorating vitals for 51 minutes during a routine foot-surgery procedure on September 13, 2024, resulting in cardiac arrest and death. Family attorneys say evidence shows a clear 'failure to rescue' — including delayed intubation and a late code call. HCA Healthcare is named directly as the responsible party. The lawsuit adds to a mounting record of individual patient-harm cases under HCA's ownership of Mission.

NC Health News / Asheville Watchdog August 25, 2026 CON

92 More Beds Up for Grabs: Four Health Systems Compete at Packed Public Hearing — While Mission Already Holds Sanctioned 95-Bed Award

More than 200 people attended a packed August 19 public hearing at A-B Tech as Mission, AdventHealth, UNC Health West, and Novant Health each pitched for up to 92 new Buncombe County hospital beds. Mission — which already received a controversial state CON award for 95 beds in March while under active federal safety sanctions — is again in the running. Competitors UNC Health and AdventHealth are appealing the earlier Mission CON decision.

How to position this
  • NC DHHS already awarded Mission 95 CON beds in March 2026 while the hospital was under active CMS Immediate Jeopardy sanctions — rewarding the worst performer in the region.
  • Granting Mission more beds before it achieves CMS compliance and submits required staffing plans rewards HCA's monopoly position without accountability.
  • State regulators and legislators must condition any new CON on full CMS compliance, submission of staffing plans, and return to NC-average FTE ratios.
  • Advocates should ask: if Mission can't safely staff 853 beds, why is it eligible to compete for 92 more?

On August 19, 2026, the NC DHHS held a public hearing at Asheville-Buncombe Technical College drawing over 200 community members, with four hospital systems — Mission, AdventHealth, UNC Health West, and Novant Health — each proposing expansions to capture up to 92 newly authorized Buncombe County beds. Mission, now licensed for 853 beds after its March 2026 CON award of 95 beds (which rivals are appealing), is seeking still more capacity. The hearing comes amid deep community distrust of state regulators for approving Mission's earlier expansion despite four Immediate Jeopardy citations and ongoing federal noncompliance.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

National Investigation: Mission's Knee Replacement Costs $40K vs. $16K Elsewhere — Hospital Monopoly Drives WNC Price Surge

A major investigative piece by Peter Whoriskey found that under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement cost roughly $40,000 at Mission versus $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center, with Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare — well above the NC benchmark of 280%. The investigation traces Mission's monopoly to the 1998 merger, HCA's 2019 purchase of a 'prepackaged monopoly,' and the 2015 repeal of state profit limits. Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC are featured.

How to position this
  • Mission charges more than twice what a comparable non-monopoly hospital charges for the same knee surgery — a direct cost imposed on WNC workers and employers.
  • HCA's 2019 acquisition of a 'prepackaged monopoly' locked in inflated prices with no competitive check; state CON policy enabled it.
  • Despite charging 334% of Medicare, HCA has cut staffing to 3.7 FTE/bed and collected four Immediate Jeopardy citations — high prices, low quality.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Mayfield are on record: 'Unregulated monopolies have never gone well for the public.'

Investigative reporter Peter Whoriskey documented how Mission Hospital's monopoly position — cemented by the 1998 St. Joseph's/Memorial Mission merger, a 2015 profit-cap repeal, and HCA's 2019 acquisition — has produced some of the highest hospital prices in North Carolina. Using RAND 2024 data, Mission's commercial prices ran 334% of Medicare versus 237% at Catawba Valley, and 334% versus NC's benchmark of 280%. Individual cases include patient Marcelle Crago and employer Katie Button; Yale economist Zack Cooper and Sen. Julie Mayfield provide policy context. Reclaim Healthcare WNC is quoted in the piece.

WLOS / Dogwood Health Trust August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent Monitor: HCA Cannot Sell Mission for 10 Years Without Consent — and Compliance Failures Persist

At a public Q&A following the 2025 HCA monitoring report, Dogwood General Counsel Rachel Ryan confirmed that under the Asset Purchase Agreement HCA is barred from selling Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board consent, and said she is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. She also restated the monitor's two findings of potential noncompliance: service discontinuation/degradation and multiple Immediate Jeopardy designations. The webinar drew more than 90 community members.

How to position this
  • The 10-year no-sale clause means HCA is locked in — community pressure and the AG lawsuit are the primary levers for forcing accountability before 2029.
  • The monitor found potential noncompliance on service cuts AND on repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations — two separate violations of the APA HCA signed in 2019.
  • Dogwood's only remedy under the APA is 'specific performance' — compelling HCA to comply — not fines or forced sale; that makes the AG lawsuit and CMS enforcement the critical backstops.
  • Advocates should demand that the monitor's noncompliance findings are formally escalated and tied to concrete compliance deadlines in the AG lawsuit proceedings.

On August 5, 2026, WLOS covered a community Q&A hosted by Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors following the release of the 2025 HCA compliance monitoring report. Monitor representative Rachel Ryan clarified that the Asset Purchase Agreement bars HCA from selling Mission during the first 10 years of ownership without advisory board consent, and said she is unaware of any HCA intent to sell. The webinar also restated the monitor's two findings of potential noncompliance: degradation of services (industrial rehab, emergency, oncology, pediatric subspecialties) and the hospital's repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations. Over 90 community members attended the August 4 webinar hosted by Dogwood and Affiliated Monitors.

NC DOJ / NC Health News July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA Summary Judgment — AG Jackson's Mission Hospital Lawsuit Advances

The NC Business Court denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, 2026, allowing AG Jeff Jackson's lawsuit to proceed. Jackson alleges HCA failed to deliver on emergency and cancer care commitments made when it acquired Mission in 2019. The denial means a full trial on HCA's conduct at Mission is now on track, a major win for accountability advocates.

How to position this
  • A summary-judgment denial means a court found enough factual dispute about HCA's conduct to warrant a full trial — this is a major legal milestone for WNC accountability.
  • The AG's allegations — failed emergency and cancer care — match exactly what the independent monitor flagged as potential APA noncompliance in its 2025 annual report.
  • HCA spent $2.1B on share buybacks in Q2 2026 while facing this lawsuit; investors need to hear that Mission liability is a material risk.
  • Advocates should amplify: HCA could not get this case thrown out — the evidence of broken promises is strong enough to go before a judge.

On July 28, 2026, the North Carolina Business Court rejected HCA's attempt to end AG Jeff Jackson's lawsuit at the summary-judgment stage, ruling that the case must proceed. The AG alleges HCA violated the Asset Purchase Agreement by failing to provide the emergency and cancer care it promised when acquiring Mission Health System in 2019. AG Jackson called it a decisive step forward in defending emergency and cancer care in western North Carolina. The same day, NC Health News reported the independent monitor's concurrent finding that HCA is in 'potential noncompliance' with the APA — reinforcing the AG's core allegations.

HCA Investor Relations / SEC 10-Q July 24, 2026 Earnings

HCA Q2 2026: $20.2B Revenue, $7.62 EPS — But Guidance Cut on Uninsured Patient Surge

HCA reported Q2 2026 revenues of $20.23 billion (up 8.7% year-over-year) and net income of $1.699 billion ($7.62 diluted EPS), but simultaneously slashed full-year 2026 earnings guidance from $6.5–$7.0B to $6.3–$6.7B net income as uninsured patient volumes — driven by loss of ACA enhanced subsidies — cut into margins. Despite the guidance cut, HCA returned $2.1 billion to shareholders via buybacks and $171 million in dividends in the quarter alone.

How to position this

  • Guidance was cut because more uninsured Americans are using HCA hospitals — the same patients Mission's reduced staffing and service cuts put at greatest risk.
  • Revised 2026 revenue guidance ($77–$79.5B) still dwarfs the investment needed to restore Mission's staffing to NC average; HCA's priorities are clear.
  • Investors heard about payer-mix risk on the earnings call — they heard nothing about Mission's four Immediate Jeopardy citations, AG lawsuit, or federal noncompliance.

HCA Healthcare reported Q2 2026 revenues of $20.23 billion and net income of $1.699 billion ($7.62 diluted EPS) on July 24, 2026, with adjusted EBITDA of $4.027 billion (margin 19.9%). However, the company cut its full-year 2026 net income guidance to $6.3–$6.7 billion (from $6.5–$7.0 billion) after a surge in uninsured patients — linked to the expiration of ACA enhanced subsidies — reduced revenue quality. Cash flow from operations fell 45% year-over-year to $2.335 billion, partly due to Medicaid payment timing. HCA still repurchased $2.1 billion in shares and paid $171 million in dividends during the quarter.

Arc — running narrative
2019–2026: HCA buys Mission with binding promises → staffing collapse and four Immediate Jeopardy citations → CON beds awarded amid sanctions → AG lawsuit headed to trial → national coverage of monopoly pricing. Read moreShow less
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
Hazen testimony
HCA CEO Sam Hazen testifies before House Ways and Means. Written testimony pushes eliminating CON laws and highlights Helene response in western NC — without addressing Mission’s Immediate Jeopardy citations, staffing collapse, or the AG lawsuit.
Jul 24, 2026
Q2 Earnings
HCA reports strong Q2 2026 results. Mission's noncompliance is not mentioned on the earnings call. "Operational efficiency" language contrasts sharply with the concurrent federal monitor finding of zero staffing plans submitted by Mission.
Jul 26, 2026
Deadline passes
The Enhanced Plan of Correction deadline expires. The federal monitor confirms Mission has not achieved substantial compliance and has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the same deficiencies driving every prior sanction.
Jul 28, 2026
Trial confirmed
Judge denies HCA's summary judgment motion. The NC Attorney General's lawsuit will go to trial. A court will now assess whether HCA honored its legal commitments to WNC. Reclaim's years of documentation, testimony, and public pressure have contributed directly to this moment.
Aug 9, 2026
National spotlight
Washington Post / KFF Health News documents Mission's monopoly pricing: ~$40K vs. ~$16K for the same Blue Cross knee replacement an hour away, and commercial rates at 334% of Medicare. The investigation features Reclaim Healthcare WNC and frames Asheville as a national warning about hospital consolidation.
What's next
Trial date TBD in NC Business Court. CMS must determine next steps following Mission's failure to achieve substantial compliance — options include continued monitoring, Medicare/Medicaid payment suspension, or termination. HCA Q3 earnings (expected October 2026) will again test whether HCA addresses Mission publicly in investor communications.
