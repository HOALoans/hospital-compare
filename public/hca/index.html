Asheville Watchdog September 10, 2026 Congress

Rep. Edwards Introduces 'HCA Mission Act' to Give CMS Financial Penalty Power Over Repeat Offenders

U.S. Rep. Chuck Edwards filed the Health Care Accountability of Mission Act (H.R. 10311) on September 8, 2026, directly naming Mission Hospital's four Immediate Jeopardy citations as the impetus. The bill would allow CMS to impose fines of $10,000 per day on hospitals that accumulate two Immediate Jeopardy designations within two years. The bipartisan pressure signals growing federal recognition that existing CMS tools have failed to hold HCA accountable.

How to position this
  • Mission is the direct legislative catalyst: four IJ citations in five years forced even a Republican congressman to call for new federal penalties.
  • HCA CEO Sam Hazen testified before Ways and Means in April 2026 urging CON elimination and touting Helene response — but said nothing about Mission's IJ record, AG lawsuit, or noncompliance finding.
  • CMS's current toolkit — termination threats only — has been a 'merry-go-round' that HCA exploits; the Edwards bill would create real financial consequences.
  • Advocates should push Ways and Means to schedule a hearing on H.R. 10311 and demand HCA leadership testify on Mission's safety record, not just PR talking points.

Congressman Chuck Edwards introduced H.R. 10311 — the Health Care Accountability of Mission Act of 2026 — on September 8, 2026, in direct response to Mission Hospital's four Immediate Jeopardy citations since HCA acquired it in 2019. The bill would amend Title XVIII of the Social Security Act to authorize CMS to levy civil monetary penalties of $10,000 per day against hospitals that repeatedly violate patient-safety standards. It was referred to the House Ways and Means Committee. The legislation marks a shift from Edwards, who two years ago said federal intervention at Mission was not viable.

More than 100 community members packed a public hearing at AB Tech on August 19 as Mission, Novant, UNC Health, and AdventHealth competed for a CON covering 92 additional acute-care beds earmarked for western NC.   • Advocates should demand DHHS require Mission to achieve sustained CMS compliance and submit a full staffing plan before any new CON is approved.
  • Hazen's April Ways and Means testimony urged eliminating CON laws nationally — while HCA was actively litigating to win more CON beds in NC.

The NC DHHS Certificate of Need process entered a new round in August 2026 with 92 additional acute-care beds on the table for western North Carolina. At a packed August 19 public hearing, all four competing health systems — Mission/HCA, Novant, UNC Health, and AdventHealth — made their pitches, but community anger was directed squarely at HCA. Critics argue awarding Mission more beds despite active federal safety sanctions rewards failure.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly Pricing

WaPo/KFF Investigation: Mission Hospital Charges More Than Double Competitors for Same Procedures

A major joint investigation by Peter Whoriskey found that under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement at Mission Hospital cost roughly $40,000 — compared to about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center. A Rand 2024 analysis placed Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare, well above the NC benchmark of 280%. The investigation traces the pricing power directly to the 1998 Asheville merger that created a regional monopoly and HCA's 2019 purchase of a 'prepackaged monopoly.'

How to position this
  • Mission charges more than double competitors for the same procedures — monopoly pricing enabled by the 1998 merger HCA bought into in 2019.
  • At 334% of Medicare in commercial prices, Mission is 54 points above the NC benchmark — a direct cost borne by every insured family and employer in western NC.
  • HCA is simultaneously cutting staff ratios to 3.7 FTE/bed (vs. 5.1 NC average) while extracting monopoly rents from patients and insurers.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Julie Mayfield are quoted in the investigation — keep sharing this story to connect hospital pricing to the AG lawsuit and staffing crisis.

An August 9, 2026 investigation by KFF Health News and the Washington Post documented severe price disparities at Mission Hospital: a knee replacement cost ~$40,000 at Mission vs. ~$16,000 at Catawba Valley; a breast biopsy $7,500 vs. $1,700; a hernia repair $17,700 vs. $9,600 (UnitedHealthcare rates). Rand 2024 data placed Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare, vs. a 237% NC comparison hospital and a 280% state benchmark. The story features patient Marcelle Crago, employer Katie Button, Yale economist Zack Cooper, and Sen. Julie Mayfield along with Reclaim Healthcare WNC.

WLOS / NC DOJ August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent Monitor: HCA Cannot Sell Mission Without Advisory Board Consent; Noncompliance Concerns Restated

At a public Q&A covered by WLOS, Dogwood/Affiliated Monitors representative Rachel Ryan confirmed that under the Asset Purchase Agreement, HCA is barred from selling Mission Hospital during the first 10 years without advisory board consent, and that she is unaware of any HCA intent to sell. Ryan also restated findings from the 2025 monitoring report: potential noncompliance on service discontinuation and Mission's multiple Immediate Jeopardy citations remain serious open concerns.

How to position this
  • HCA is contractually barred from selling Mission for 10 years without advisory board consent — ruling out a quick exit strategy that might otherwise relieve accountability pressure.
  • The monitor's 2025 report flagged two potential noncompliance areas: service discontinuation and repeated Immediate Jeopardy — both remain unresolved.
  • The monitor acknowledged prior difficulties getting information from HCA, underscoring the weak enforcement tools Dogwood has outside of court action.
  • The AG lawsuit proceeding to trial is now the primary enforcement mechanism — community members should support the AG's office and engage the monitoring process.

A community Q&A session on August 5, 2026, covered by WLOS, featured Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors discussing HCA's ongoing compliance with the 2019 Mission acquisition agreement. Monitor representative Rachel Ryan confirmed HCA cannot sell Mission in the first 10 years without advisory board consent and is unaware of any sale plans. Ryan also restated the 2025 monitoring report's two potential noncompliance findings: service discontinuation and the pattern of Immediate Jeopardy citations. The session followed a community webinar hosted by Dogwood on August 4.

  • The monitor's noncompliance finding and the court ruling reinforce each other — HCA faces accountability on two parallel tracks simultaneously.
  • Community members, patients, and local employers should stay engaged: the trial is the best opportunity to hold HCA legally accountable for its conduct at Mission.

Arc — running narrative
2019–2026: HCA buys Mission with binding promises → staffing collapse and four Immediate Jeopardy citations → CON beds awarded amid sanctions → AG lawsuit headed to trial → national coverage of monopoly pricing. Read moreShow less
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
Hazen testimony
HCA CEO Sam Hazen testifies before House Ways and Means. Written testimony pushes eliminating CON laws and highlights Helene response in western NC — without addressing Mission’s Immediate Jeopardy citations, staffing collapse, or the AG lawsuit.
Jul 24, 2026
Q2 Earnings
HCA reports strong Q2 2026 results. Mission's noncompliance is not mentioned on the earnings call. "Operational efficiency" language contrasts sharply with the concurrent federal monitor finding of zero staffing plans submitted by Mission.
Jul 26, 2026
Deadline passes
The Enhanced Plan of Correction deadline expires. The federal monitor confirms Mission has not achieved substantial compliance and has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the same deficiencies driving every prior sanction.
Jul 28, 2026
Trial confirmed
Judge denies HCA's summary judgment motion. The NC Attorney General's lawsuit will go to trial. A court will now assess whether HCA honored its legal commitments to WNC. Reclaim's years of documentation, testimony, and public pressure have contributed directly to this moment.
