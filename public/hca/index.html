KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

'Same Knee Surgery, Twice the Price': National Investigation Exposes Mission Hospital's Monopoly Pricing

A major investigation by KFF Health News and the Washington Post found that under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement costs roughly $40,000 at Mission Hospital versus about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center — more than double. The investigation traces the problem from Mission's 1998 merger to HCA's 2019 acquisition of what one economist called a 'prepackaged monopoly.' Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Julie Mayfield are quoted in the piece.

How to position this
  • Mission charges 334% of Medicare rates — 54 points above the NC benchmark and more than twice competing hospitals for identical procedures.
  • HCA purchased a 'prepackaged monopoly' in 2019 and has leveraged that market power to maximize extraction from WNC patients and employers.
  • A knee replacement costs $40,000 at Mission under the same Blue Cross plan that prices the same surgery at $16,000 at Catawba Valley — a gap that falls entirely on WNC families.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Julie Mayfield are named in the investigation, amplifying the case that federal and state accountability are both overdue.

Investigative reporter Peter Whoriskey found that Mission Hospital's commercial prices reached 334% of Medicare rates — well above the 237% at Catawba Valley and the 280% NC benchmark, per Rand 2024 data. Under a UnitedHealthcare plan, a breast biopsy ran $7,500 at Mission vs. $1,700 elsewhere, and a hernia repair $17,700 vs. $9,600. Yale economist Zack Cooper, patient Marcelle Crago, and employer Katie Button are featured alongside Sen. Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC. The story traces Mission's monopoly to its 1998 regional merger, a 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of an already-dominant system.

NC Department of Justice July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA Summary Judgment; AG Jackson's Lawsuit Against HCA Over Mission Hospital Proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, 2026, ruling that the AG had produced sufficient evidence that HCA failed to meet its APA commitments on emergency and cancer care — and that HCA's narrow 'complete elimination' interpretation was rejected. AG Jeff Jackson's office stated: the lawsuit proceeds, and Jackson will keep fighting to defend emergency and cancer care in western North Carolina.

How to position this
  • A judge has ruled HCA must actually deliver on its emergency and cancer care promises — not merely avoid total elimination of services.
  • HCA's legal argument that only complete service elimination would constitute a breach was rejected by the court, a major win for accountability.
  • With four Immediate Jeopardy citations since 2019, the record before the court reflects a pattern — not isolated failures.
  • The AG lawsuit now proceeds: WNC residents, lawmakers, and advocates should press for swift trial scheduling and full public transparency.

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp ruled largely in favor of AG Jeff Jackson's arguments, finding that HCA's Asset Purchase Agreement requires it to 'take all steps necessary to actually provide' emergency and cancer services — not merely avoid eliminating them entirely. HCA's motion for summary judgment was denied, clearing the lawsuit for trial. The court did find that the AG had not produced evidence of a missing ENT surgeon in the emergency department as required for Level 2 trauma status. CMS has found Mission in Immediate Jeopardy four times since 2019, most recently in January 2026.

Asheville Watchdog July 2, 2026 Staffing

Mission Hospital Drops BSN Requirement for Nurses Amid Ongoing Staffing Crisis

Beginning July 2026, Mission Hospital no longer requires newly hired nurses to hold a four-year BSN degree or obtain one within five years — accepting a two-year associate degree instead. The move is framed by HCA as a recruitment tool, but comes after more than 600 nurses have left Mission since the 2019 acquisition and as the hospital's staffing ratio has fallen to 3.7 FTE per bed against a NC average of 5.1.

How to position this
  • Dropping the BSN requirement is an admission that HCA's staffing practices have so depleted Mission's workforce that the hospital cannot attract adequately credentialed nurses.
  • Mission's FTE-per-bed ratio has collapsed from 6.1 to 3.7 — the credential rollback is a symptom of that collapse, not a solution to it.
  • 600+ nurses have left since 2019; loosening academic standards does not replace experienced clinical staff or rebuild institutional knowledge.
  • HCA has not submitted staffing improvement plans to the federal monitor — this credential change is not a substitute for the structural investment Mission needs.

Mission Hospital announced it would drop its longstanding BSN requirement for nurses, now accepting associate degree holders. The change is presented as a workforce expansion effort, but critics note it comes after years of HCA-era staffing cuts that drove experienced, higher-credentialed nurses out of the hospital. Mission's FTE-per-bed ratio has fallen from 6.1 at acquisition to 3.7, far below the NC average of 5.1. The policy shift reflects how deeply staffing capacity has eroded rather than a proactive workforce investment.

Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026

Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
