Blue Ridge Public Radio August 20, 2026 CON

Four Health Systems Compete for 92 New Buncombe County Beds — Mission Wants More Despite Ongoing Safety Record

More than 200 community members packed a public hearing at A-B Tech on August 19 as Mission Hospital, AdventHealth, UNC Health West, and Novant Health each proposed expansions targeting up to 92 new acute-care beds in Buncombe County. Mission — which already won 95 state-approved beds in April amid active federal sanctions — is again seeking 92 more beds at a proposed cost of $66.8 million. A decision is expected in late 2026.

How to position this
  • State regulators granted Mission 95 beds in April while the hospital was under active CMS sanctions — now Mission is back for 92 more, raising the same unanswered accountability questions.
  • North Carolina's CON process should require demonstrated safe staffing and patient-safety compliance before expanding a hospital's licensed capacity.
  • Three competing health systems have now entered the Asheville market, a direct consequence of Mission's failure to serve the community — HCA's monopoly grip may finally be loosening.
  • Advocates should press DHHS: no new CON approvals for Mission until the hospital meets NC average staffing ratios and clears its federal noncompliance record.

A standing-room public hearing on August 19, 2026 revealed four competing hospital systems vying for up to 92 new acute-care beds in Buncombe County. Mission Hospital — already approved for 95 beds in April despite four Immediate Jeopardy citations — is seeking an additional 92 beds totaling 869 acute-care beds at a cost of $66.8 million. UNC Health's proposal would mark its first Asheville hospital, putting it in direct competition with HCA-owned Mission. Community members questioned whether Mission should be rewarded with more capacity given its ongoing safety and staffing record.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly Pricing

Investigation: Mission Hospital Charges 2.5× More Than Competitors — A Blueprint for HCA's Monopoly Playbook

A major KFF Health News / Washington Post investigation by Peter Whoriskey finds that under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement at Mission costs roughly $40,000 versus $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center — more than double. RAND 2024 data shows Mission charges 334% of Medicare rates versus the 237% NC benchmark of 280%. The piece traces the roots of Mission's monopoly to a 1998 state-authorized merger and HCA's 2019 purchase of what the investigation calls a 'prepackaged monopoly.'

How to position this
  • Mission's monopoly pricing — 334% of Medicare vs. a 280% NC benchmark — is a direct tax on every employer and family in western NC, enabled by HCA's 2019 acquisition of a 'prepackaged monopoly.'
  • A knee replacement at Mission costs $40,000 under the same Blue Cross plan that prices the same procedure at $16,000 at a comparable NC hospital — that gap is the cost of HCA's market dominance.
  • HCA collected $20.2B in Q2 2026 revenue and bought back $2.06B in shares while western NC residents pay the highest hospital prices in the state.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Julie Mayfield are named in the investigation — use this national platform to amplify calls for price transparency and AG lawsuit accountability.

The KFF Health News / Washington Post investigation documents how Mission Hospital's near-monopoly over Asheville-area care drives prices to among the highest in North Carolina. Under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement runs ~$40,000 at Mission but only ~$16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center; a UnitedHealthcare breast biopsy costs $7,500 vs. $1,700 elsewhere. RAND 2024 commercial price data puts Mission at 334% of Medicare — far above NC's 280% benchmark. Yale economist Zack Cooper, employer Katie Button, patient Marcelle Crago, and Sen. Julie Mayfield of Reclaim Healthcare WNC are featured voices.

WLOS August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent Monitor: HCA Cannot Sell Mission Without Advisory Board Consent — Noncompliance Findings Stand

Independent monitor representative Rachel Ryan told the public on August 5 that under the 2019 Asset Purchase Agreement, HCA is prohibited from selling Mission during its first 10 years without advisory board consent, and that she is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. Ryan also restated the 2025 monitoring report's finding of potential noncompliance tied to service discontinuation at CarePartners and Mission's multiple Immediate Jeopardy designations.

How to position this
  • The APA's no-sale clause offers no comfort if HCA continues degrading Mission's services — 'can't sell' is not the same as 'must care.'
  • Two areas of potential noncompliance confirmed by the independent monitor — service cuts and repeated IJ citations — go directly to HCA's broken acquisition commitments.
  • The public deserved these answers in 2019, not in 2026 after 800+ clinical staff have departed and Mission has received 4 Immediate Jeopardy citations.
  • Advocates should demand the monitor escalate 'potential noncompliance' to formal findings and refer them directly to the AG's active lawsuit.

In an August 5 WLOS report following the August 4 community Q&A webinar, independent monitor representative Rachel Ryan clarified that the Asset Purchase Agreement bars HCA from selling Mission Hospital during the first 10 years of ownership without advisory board consent. Ryan said she is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. She also restated that the 2025 annual monitoring report identified two areas of potential noncompliance: service cuts at CarePartners and Mission's repeated Immediate Jeopardy citations since the 2019 acquisition.

NC Department of Justice / BPR July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA Summary Judgment — AG Jackson's Mission Hospital Lawsuit Proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp ruled on July 28 that AG Jeff Jackson's lawsuit against HCA over Mission Hospital will proceed, denying HCA's motion to avoid trial. The lawsuit, originally filed in 2023 by then-AG Josh Stein, alleges HCA violated commitments made at the time of its 2019 acquisition — including emergency and cancer care obligations. Separately, the independent monitor reported two areas of potential noncompliance with the Asset Purchase Agreement on the same day.

How to position this
  • A judge has now ruled that HCA must face trial for allegedly breaking its promises to western NC — this is the most significant legal accountability moment since the 2019 acquisition.
  • HCA spent millions on lobbyists and lawyers to avoid this day; the community, advocates, and two Attorneys General kept the pressure on.
  • The monitor's noncompliance findings — released the same day as the summary judgment ruling — strengthen the evidentiary record for the AG lawsuit.
  • Advocates: use 'the lawsuit proceeds' language in every public comment, CON proceeding, and legislative hearing — HCA is not exonerated, it is on trial.

On July 28, 2026, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment, allowing AG Jeff Jackson's lawsuit to proceed. The lawsuit was initially filed in 2023 under then-AG Josh Stein and accuses HCA of failing to honor acquisition commitments on emergency and cancer care. The judge ruled in favor of the AG on key arguments while granting HCA a narrow win on the ENT-surgeon claim. The same day, Dogwood Health Trust notified the AG that the independent monitor found two areas of potential noncompliance in its 2025 annual report.

HCA Healthcare Investor Relations July 24, 2026 Earnings

HCA Q2 2026: $20.2B Revenue, $2.06B in Buybacks — Even as Payer Mix Shift Hits Margins

HCA reported Q2 2026 revenue of $20.23 billion (up 8.7% YoY) and net income of $1.70 billion ($7.62 diluted EPS, up 11.6% YoY), while repurchasing 4.752 million shares at a cost of $2.064 billion. The company flagged an unfavorable payer mix shift driven by patients losing ACA exchange coverage, which it estimates cost approximately $400 million in pretax income during the quarter. Same-facility admissions rose 2.5% but inpatient and outpatient surgeries declined.

  • HCA's Q2 earnings call made no mention of Mission's AG lawsuit, four Immediate Jeopardy citations, or potential APA noncompliance — IR silence on material legal risk is itself an accountability issue.
  • The same quarter HCA spent $2B on buybacks, its western NC flagship hospital was under a CMS Enhanced Plan of Correction and facing an active state Attorney General lawsuit.

HCA Healthcare's Q2 2026 results, reported July 24, showed revenue of $20.23 billion (up 8.7% from Q2 2025) and net income of $1.699 billion ($7.62 diluted EPS). The company spent $2.064 billion repurchasing 4.752 million shares — leaving $7.21 billion remaining under its buyback authorization. A payer mix shift from ACA exchange coverage losses cost an estimated $400 million in pretax income. Adjusted EBITDA rose 4.6% to $4.03 billion. HCA simultaneously revised full-year 2026 guidance downward, citing continued ACA and Medicaid headwinds.

HCA Cuts 2026 Full-Year Guidance — ACA Coverage Losses Could Cost Up to $1.1B

HCA revised its 2026 full-year guidance downward on July 24, now projecting net income of $6.3–$6.7 billion (down from $6.5–$7.0 billion) and diluted EPS of $28.70–$30.50 (down from $29.10–$31.50).   • HCA's 'multi-year resilience plan' is cost-cutting language — communities should watch for service reductions at Mission marketed as efficiency measures.
  • Even with guidance cuts, HCA projects net income of up to $6.7B in 2026 — more than enough to restore safe staffing at Mission and honor every APA commitment made in 2019.

Acquisition

The exodus

2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025.
