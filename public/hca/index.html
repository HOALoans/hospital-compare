NC Health News / Carolina Public Press September 15, 2026 CON

HCA's Three-Year Legal War Blocked WNC Hospital Beds — None of 222 Approved Beds Have Broken Ground

A new investigative series from Carolina Public Press finds that all 222 hospital beds approved for Buncombe County over four years remain stalled — largely because of HCA's serial legal challenges to competitors. HCA held up AdventHealth's Weaverville hospital for three years in court; Mission continues as the only hospital in Buncombe County. The series also notes Mission is still appealing 26 additional beds awarded to AdventHealth in 2024, a fight now two years old with no resolution.

How to position this
  • HCA used three years of litigation to block a competing hospital while Mission accumulated four Immediate Jeopardy citations — monopoly protection at patients' expense.
  • None of the 222 beds approved for Buncombe County since 2022 have broken ground; HCA's CON battles are a documented cause of this delay.
  • Mission is still appealing 26 AdventHealth beds two years in — using legal resources to suppress competition rather than fix staffing or patient safety.
  • Hazen testified to Congress in April 2026 that CON laws harm competition; HCA's own legal playbook tells a different story in WNC.

A first installment in Carolina Public Press's 'Battling for Beds' investigative series documents how certificate-of-need appeals by industry players — led prominently by HCA — have stalled every one of the 222 hospital beds approved for development in Buncombe County since 2022. HCA successfully delayed AdventHealth's Weaverville hospital for three years; the NC Supreme Court finally rejected HCA's final appeal in December 2025. Mission, which already won 95 CON beds in March despite federal safety sanctions, is still appealing 26 additional beds awarded to AdventHealth. The series underscores that Mission remains the sole hospital serving Buncombe County — the most populous mountain county in the state.

HCA Announces $32M+ WNC Facility Investment — But No Word on Staffing Plans or Federal Noncompliance

  • Four Immediate Jeopardy citations since 2019 reflect systemic safety failures that equipment upgrades alone cannot fix.
  • HCA spent $2.06 billion buying back its own stock in Q2 2026 alone — the $32M WNC 'investment' is a rounding error compared to shareholder returns.

HCA Healthcare announced on September 14, 2026 that it will invest more than $32 million across Mission Health hospitals and facilities in western North Carolina — roughly $15 million for imaging and diagnostic technology and $13 million for surgical and cardiovascular care. KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Safety

National Investigation: Mission Hospital Charges $40K for Knee Replacement That Costs $16K Elsewhere — Monopoly Pricing Laid Bare

A major investigation by KFF Health News and the Washington Post found that under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement at Mission Hospital costs roughly $40,000 versus about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center. RAND 2024 data shows Mission charges commercial payers 334% of Medicare versus a NC benchmark of 280%. The investigation traces the pricing power to Mission's 1998 merger, the 2015 repeal of NC profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of what one expert called a 'prepackaged monopoly.'

How to position this
  • Mission charges commercial payers 334% of Medicare — 54 points above NC's 280% benchmark — a direct cost of HCA's monopoly on WNC healthcare.
  • The same knee replacement costs more than twice as much at Mission as at a comparable NC hospital under the same insurance plan — patients and employers pay the price.
  • HCA bought a 'prepackaged monopoly' in 2019 and has used it to extract above-market prices while patient experience ratings stay at 2 stars and staffing falls.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Mayfield are named in the national investigation as the community voice holding HCA accountable for monopoly pricing.

Investigative reporter Peter Whoriskey, writing for KFF Health News and the Washington Post, documented stark price disparities at Mission Hospital rooted in HCA's regional monopoly. Under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement at Mission ran approximately $40,000 versus $16,000 at Catawba Valley; a breast biopsy under UnitedHealthcare cost $7,500 at Mission vs. $1,700 elsewhere; a hernia repair $17,700 vs. $9,600. RAND 2024 commercial pricing data shows Mission at 334% of Medicare, well above NC's 280% benchmark. The piece features patient Marcelle Crago, employer Katie Button, Yale economist Zack Cooper, and Sen. Julie Mayfield of Reclaim Healthcare WNC, who calls Asheville 'a cautionary tale' on unregulated hospital monopolies.

NC DOJ / Blue Ridge Public Radio July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA Summary Judgment — AG Jackson's Mission Hospital Lawsuit Proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, 2026, ruling in favor of several of AG Jeff Jackson's arguments and finding that a trial is needed to resolve whether HCA violated its Mission acquisition commitments on emergency and cancer care. The AG's office argued HCA decreased the level and quality of services in breach of the APA; HCA countered that only complete service elimination would constitute a breach. Judge Earp ruled each side's interpretation is reasonable — putting the question to trial.

How to position this
  • A judge has now twice declined to let HCA off the hook — the court found HCA's core defense that only full service elimination counts as a breach is not the only reasonable reading of the APA.
  • The AG's lawsuit is advancing because the evidence of degraded emergency and cancer care is substantial enough to require a jury or bench trial.
  • HCA's legal strategy — deny, delay, seek summary judgment — failed; WNC patients will now get their day in court.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and community advocates have maintained public pressure that helped sustain the political will to bring and pursue this lawsuit.

On July 28, 2026, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied HCA Healthcare's motion for summary judgment in the AG's lawsuit over Mission Hospital, ruling that the case must proceed. AG Jeff Jackson's office argued HCA breached its 2019 acquisition commitments by degrading emergency and cancer care; HCA argued only a complete service elimination would constitute a breach. Judge Earp found both interpretations reasonable and said a trial is necessary to resolve the dispute. The lawsuit was originally filed in 2023 by then-AG Josh Stein and has continued under Jackson's office.
