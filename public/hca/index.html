Asheville Watchdog September 17, 2026 Update

HCA CEO Makes First Asheville Appearance, Pledges Mission Improvement — Behind Closed Doors

HCA Healthcare CEO Sam Hazen made a surprise appearance at an Aug. 28 closed meeting of the WNC Health Policy Initiative at MAHEC, his first public acknowledgment that Mission Hospital needed to perform better. He claimed HCA has invested nearly $750 million in Mission since 2019, offered no concrete staffing commitments, and took no questions from the broader public. The meeting had roughly 20 attendees — no advance notice was given, citing security concerns.

How to position this
  • Hazen's 'closed door' format excluded patients, nurses, and the public — accountability requires transparency, not invite-only sessions with 20 stakeholders.
  • No concrete staffing ratios, timeline, or measurable targets were announced — 'we're committed to fixing that' is the same pledge HCA has made for seven years.
  • The meeting came only after a federal monitor found Mission out of compliance, the AG lawsuit survived summary judgment, and WaPo/KFF published a national monopoly-pricing exposé — Hazen did not come voluntarily.
  • HCA's claim of $750M invested since 2019 cannot be independently verified and has not translated into improved patient experience (2-star HCAHPS) or safe staffing ratios.

HCA CEO Sam Hazen surprised roughly 20 community health leaders at an Aug. 28 invitation-only meeting at the Mountain Area Health Education Center — the first time he publicly acknowledged Mission's persistent performance failures. Hazen claimed HCA has invested nearly $750 million in Mission since acquisition and described a three-part strategy around governance, workforce, and systems. He made no specific staffing commitments and the meeting was not open to the public or press. Sen. Julie Mayfield, attending virtually, called the appearance 'different and better' while advocates note it came only amid a federal monitor finding, an advancing AG lawsuit, and sustained public pressure.

NC Health News / Carolina Public Press September 15, 2026 CON

HCA's Legal Tactics Delayed WNC Hospital Beds for Years — Pattern Documented Statewide

A Carolina Public Press investigation published by NC Health News documents how HCA's legal challenges to AdventHealth's approved Weaverville hospital delayed construction for years, protecting Mission's monopoly over Buncombe County. The same CON-appeal playbook is holding up hundreds of approved beds statewide. Meanwhile, HCA CEO Sam Hazen urged Congress in April to eliminate CON laws — while Mission retained 95 newly approved CON beds amid active federal safety sanctions.

How to position this
  • HCA sued to block a competitor approved by the state — then HCA CEO Sam Hazen told Congress in April that CON laws should be eliminated, a position that benefits Mission only after locking in its monopoly.
  • Patients in WNC went without a local alternative to Mission Hospital for years because HCA weaponized the legal appeals process to suppress competition.
  • NC DHHS awarded Mission 95 additional CON beds in March 2026 even as the hospital faced active CMS sanctions — rewarding noncompliance with expansion authority.
  • HCA's use of CON appeals is a documented playbook: block competition, raise prices, cut services — exactly what the WaPo/KFF investigation and NNU Too Big to Care case study describe.

A Sept. 15 Carolina Public Press investigation reveals how HCA used North Carolina's certificate-of-need appeals process to block AdventHealth's state-approved Weaverville hospital for roughly three years, delaying construction until 2026. The pattern is documented statewide: hundreds of approved beds remain tied up in industry-driven appeals. HCA's strategy preserved Mission's geographic monopoly while the hospital accumulated four Immediate Jeopardy citations. The piece is part of a three-part 'Battling for Beds' investigative series.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

National Investigation: Mission Hospital Charges Double — HCA's 'Prepackaged Monopoly' at the Center of U.S. Hospital Pricing Crisis

A major KFF Health News/Washington Post investigation by Peter Whoriskey places Mission Hospital at the center of America's hospital monopoly-pricing crisis. Under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement cost roughly $40,000 at Mission versus $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center; RAND data shows Mission's commercial rates at 334% of Medicare versus a 280% NC benchmark. The piece traces the 1998 merger that built Mission's monopoly, the 2015 repeal of NC profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of what one Yale economist called a 'prepackaged monopoly.'

How to position this
  • Mission charges patients and employers double or more for common procedures — not because of quality (2-star HCAHPS), but because it faces no competition after HCA bought a 'prepackaged monopoly.'
  • RAND data: Mission's commercial prices are 334% of Medicare vs. a 280% NC benchmark and 237% at comparable Catawba Valley Medical Center — monopoly pricing documented with federal disclosure data.
  • Employers, workers, and insurers in WNC pay a hidden Mission monopoly tax — and HCA extracts those profits while Mission remains federally noncompliant on staffing and safety.
  • Sen. Julie Mayfield's warning is now national news: 'Unregulated monopolies have never gone well for the public' — Asheville is the cautionary tale for pending hospital mergers nationwide.

A landmark Aug. 9 joint investigation by KFF Health News and The Washington Post examines how hospital consolidation drives up prices, featuring Mission Hospital as a central case study. Reporters found a knee replacement cost about $40,000 at Mission versus roughly $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center under the same insurer, and RAND 2024 data shows Mission's commercial rates at 334% of Medicare — well above the 280% NC benchmark. The article features patient Marcelle Crago, Asheville employer Katie Button, Yale economist Zack Cooper, and Sen. Julie Mayfield of Reclaim Healthcare WNC. It traces Mission's monopoly roots to a 1998 merger, a 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and HCA's acquisition of a 'prepackaged monopoly' in 2019.

WLOS August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent Monitor: HCA Obligated Not to Sell Mission for 10 Years; 2025 Noncompliance Concerns Restated

At a public Q&A hosted by Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors on Aug. 4–5, monitor representative Rachel Ryan clarified that under the Asset Purchase Agreement HCA cannot sell Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board consent, and said she is unaware of any HCA intent to sell. Ryan restated the 2025 monitoring report's findings of potential noncompliance related to service discontinuation and multiple Immediate Jeopardy designations.

How to position this
  • The independent monitor publicly restated potential noncompliance on service cuts and four Immediate Jeopardy citations — confirming advocates' core claims are now formally documented by the oversight body HCA agreed to.
  • HCA cannot sell Mission for the first 10 years without advisory board consent — but the question of whether HCA is running Mission into the ground while locked into ownership deserves scrutiny.
  • The monitor's 90+ attendee webinar shows the community is paying close attention; HCA's continued non-disclosure of data to the monitor is itself a red flag for accountability.
  • Potential noncompliance on 'service discontinuation' echoes the AG lawsuit's core allegation — that Mission has effectively eliminated emergency and oncology services required under the sale agreement.

Following an Aug. 4 community webinar co-hosted by Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors, WLOS reported on monitor representative Rachel Ryan's public statements about Mission's ownership and compliance status. Ryan confirmed that under the Asset Purchase Agreement, HCA is obligated not to sell Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board consent, and said she is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. She restated the 2025 monitoring report's concerns about potential noncompliance tied to service discontinuation and repeated Immediate Jeopardy citations — findings that later contributed to the July 28 AG lawsuit ruling.

NC DOJ / Asheville Watchdog July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

AG Lawsuit Advances: Judge Denies HCA Summary Judgment; Dogwood Flags 'Potential Noncompliance'

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, ruling in favor of several of AG Jeff Jackson's key arguments and allowing the lawsuit over Mission Hospital's service failures to proceed. The same day, Dogwood Health Trust notified the AG's office that HCA is in 'potential noncompliance' with its 2019 acquisition commitments, citing repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations and suspected service discontinuations.

How to position this
  • The AG lawsuit survives summary judgment — HCA could not get the case thrown out, and the court found the state's core claims legally viable. This is a major win for accountability.
  • Dogwood's formal 'potential noncompliance' notification to the AG on the same day as the ruling creates a convergence of legal, regulatory, and contractual pressure on HCA simultaneously.
  • HCA has failed to submit required staffing plans to the federal monitor and has withheld data — the monitor's noncompliance finding adds federal weight to the state-level legal fight.
  • Seven years after acquisition, Mission faces an advancing AG lawsuit, federal noncompliance, and a formal potential-noncompliance notice from its own oversight body — all at once.

On July 28, 2026, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp largely denied HCA's motion for summary judgment, ruling in AG Jeff Jackson's favor on several key arguments. The lawsuit — which alleges HCA violated acquisition commitments to maintain emergency and oncology service levels — now proceeds toward trial. On the same day, Dogwood Health Trust formally notified the AG's office that HCA is in 'potential noncompliance' with the Asset Purchase Agreement, citing four Immediate Jeopardy citations and what the independent monitor described as potential service discontinuation. The federal monitor separately confirmed Mission is not in substantial compliance with its Enhanced Plan of Correction.

HCA Q2 2026: $20.2B Revenue, $7.62 EPS — Guidance Cut, $2.06B in Buybacks

Healthcare Dive July 14, 2026 Guidance

HCA Slashes 2026 Earnings Forecast on ACA Coverage Losses — Stock Falls Sharply

HCA Healthcare cut its 2026 earnings guidance on July 14, citing ACA exchange enrollment losses that cost the company roughly $400 million, worsening payer mix, and declining surgical volumes. The company lowered full-year net income guidance to $6.3B–$6.7B from a prior range of $6.495B–$7.035B. HCA's stock fell sharply on the news, marking its fifth consecutive day of losses.

How to position this
  • Even after slashing guidance, HCA expects $6.3B–$6.7B in net income in 2026 — profit at that scale coexists with Mission's 3.7 FTE/bed staffing ratio and federal noncompliance. 'Financial pressure' is not an excuse for unsafe care.
  • HCA attributes the shortfall to uninsured patients losing ACA coverage — many of those uninsured patients are in communities HCA serves as the only hospital. Mission serves the most medically vulnerable in WNC.
  • Declining surgical volumes at a hospital with 2-star patient ratings and a documented staffing collapse should raise red flags for investors — but HCA's IR materials do not mention Mission's safety record.
  • HCA's guidance cut does not alter its capital return priorities: buybacks and dividends continue as Mission's federally required staffing plans remain unsubmitted to monitors.

On July 14, 2026, HCA Healthcare preannounced a guidance cut ahead of its formal Q2 earnings, lowering full-year net income guidance from $6.495B–$7.035B to $6.3B–$6.7B after ACA exchange coverage losses cut approximately $400 million from its expected revenue. The company also flagged declining surgical volumes and a worse-than-expected payer mix shift. HCA's stock fell sharply, marking its fifth consecutive session of losses. Despite the cut, HCA maintained billions in buyback capacity and dividend payments, and projected full-year revenues of $77B–$79.5B.

2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
