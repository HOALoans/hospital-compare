Asheville Watchdog August 26, 2026 Safety

Patient Death Lawsuit Filed Against Mission as Hospital Claims CMS 'Substantial Compliance'

A new lawsuit alleges a nurse anesthetist failed to intervene as 64-year-old Noe Serrano Hernandez suffered a fatal heart attack during routine foot surgery at Mission Hospital in September 2024. The filing arrives days after Mission CEO Greg Lowe told staff CMS had found the hospital in 'substantial compliance' following its fourth Immediate Jeopardy — a claim advocates say obscures the underlying pattern of care failures.

How to position this
  • A father died during routine foot surgery while care staff allegedly failed to monitor deteriorating vitals — another preventable death at a hospital with four Immediate Jeopardy designations since HCA's purchase.
  • HCA's 'substantial compliance' milestone does not address underlying staffing ratios that have collapsed from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE per bed, nor the missing staffing plans flagged by the federal monitor in July 2026.
  • CMS substantial compliance ends additional EPoC surveys but cannot erase 4 IJ citations, 9 patient deaths, 2-star HCAHPS ratings every year 2020–2024, and the active AG lawsuit.
  • Ask HCA: if Mission is now compliant, why has it not filed the staffing plans required under the July 2026 federal monitor report?

The lawsuit alleges that on September 13, 2024, Noe Serrano Hernandez was placed under anesthesia for a routine foot surgery, and over the next 51 minutes his vital signs steadily weakened while the supervising nurse anesthetist did not intervene until he suffered a cardiac arrest. The filing comes as Mission simultaneously announced that CMS had found the hospital back in substantial compliance following the Enhanced Plan of Correction required after its January 2026 Immediate Jeopardy. Advocates note that CMS 'substantial compliance' is a regulatory floor — not a restoration of patient safety — and that HCA still has not submitted court-required staffing plans. Mission has now accumulated four Immediate Jeopardy designations and nine associated patient deaths or harms since HCA's 2019 acquisition.

NC Health News / Asheville Watchdog August 25, 2026 CON

Community Anger at HCA Dominates Hearing on 92 New WNC Hospital Beds

More than 100 residents packed Asheville-Buncombe Technical College's Ferguson Auditorium on August 19 to comment on competing Certificate of Need applications from Mission, AdventHealth, Novant, and UNC Health West for 92 additional beds in Buncombe County. Speakers directed most of their frustration at HCA, whose Mission Hospital already won 95 CON beds in March amid active federal safety sanctions and now seeks more. State regulators must decide before end of 2026.

How to position this
  • Community members told state regulators plainly: 'Mission Hospital should not get one more bed' — a verdict born from four Immediate Jeopardy findings and nine patient deaths under HCA ownership.
  • Mission's CON application projects adding beds with almost no new staff in trauma care, security, or nursing administration — institutionalizing a staffing model regulators and the federal monitor have already found dangerous.
  • NC DHHS awarded Mission 95 CON beds in March 2026 even as the hospital was under active federal safety sanctions — the new 92-bed round gives regulators a chance to reverse that pattern.
  • State CON policy should require safe staffing plans as a condition of any further Mission bed approvals; HCA's track record makes more beds without more staff a public safety risk.

Four health systems — HCA/Mission, AdventHealth, Novant, and UNC Health West — are competing for 92 new beds covering Buncombe, Clay, Graham, Madison, and Yancey counties, with all four proposing to place beds in Buncombe. More than 200 people attended the August 19 public hearing, with community commentary overwhelmingly focused on distrust of HCA and Mission's safety record. Mission is already licensed for 853 beds after the disputed March 2026 CON award of 95 beds, which AdventHealth and UNC Health are contesting on appeal. Advocates note that Mission's own CON application projects only 3.7% staff growth while beds expand nearly 13%, compounding the staffing crisis.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

WaPo/KFF Investigation: Mission Hospital Charges 2× Competitors for Same Procedures Under HCA Monopoly

A major investigation by Peter Whoriskey documents how Mission Hospital's HCA-backed monopoly position enables it to charge roughly twice the price of regional competitors for common procedures: a knee replacement runs about $40,000 at Mission versus ~$16,000 at Catawba Valley, and Mission's commercial prices reached 334% of Medicare versus a 237% NC comparator. The piece traces the origins to Mission's 1998 consolidation, the 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of what one economist calls a 'prepackaged monopoly.' Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC are featured.

How to position this
  • Mission's monopoly pricing — 334% of Medicare vs. 237% at a comparable NC hospital — is HCA's business model, not a market outcome: HCA bought a 'prepackaged monopoly' and immediately moved to extract it.
  • The same surgery, the same insurer, 2–3× the price: WNC families and employers pay HCA's monopoly premium while Mission scores 2 stars on patient experience every year from 2020–2024.
  • Sen. Mayfield's framing is apt: 'Unregulated monopolies have never gone well for the public' — and the AG lawsuit is the mechanism to enforce the purchase commitments HCA made when it bought that monopoly.
  • Ask HCA: if Mission's prices are justified by quality, why does it carry the lowest HCAHPS patient-experience ratings of all CON applicants and four Immediate Jeopardy designations?

The investigation uses insurance pricing data to show that under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement costs about $40,000 at Mission and roughly $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center; a UnitedHealthcare breast biopsy runs $7,500 vs. $1,700, and a hernia repair $17,700 vs. $9,600. Rand 2024 data put Mission's commercial rates at 334% of Medicare, far above the 240% NC benchmark and the 237% rate at Catawba Valley. Yale economist Zack Cooper attributes the price gap to Mission's regional monopoly, which dates to a 1998 merger, was entrenched by the 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and was then acquired by HCA — a company with a documented strategy of buying and exploiting regional dominance. Patient Marcelle Crago and employer Katie Button illustrate how the pricing affects real WNC families and businesses.

WLOS August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent Monitor: HCA Contractually Barred from Selling Mission for 10 Years; Potential Noncompliance Stands

During the Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors public Q&A webinar (August 4, 90+ attendees), monitor representative Rachel Ryan confirmed that HCA is obligated under the Asset Purchase Agreement not to sell Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board consent, and that she is unaware of any HCA intent to sell. Ryan also restated the 2025 monitoring report findings of potential noncompliance on service discontinuation and multiple Immediate Jeopardy designations — with no resolution in sight.

How to position this
  • HCA cannot sell Mission without advisory board consent for the first 10 years — but being trapped in the market is no comfort to patients suffering under a 3.7 FTE-per-bed staffing ratio.
  • The monitor's potential noncompliance findings on service cuts (pediatric subspecialties, industrial rehab) are unresolved — HCA has acknowledged the issues without remediation plans.
  • The July 2026 monitor report confirmed Mission has not submitted required staffing plans — a direct violation of the purchase commitments HCA made to WNC in 2019.
  • Community Q&A attendance of 90+ shows WNC residents remain deeply engaged; advocates should direct neighbors to the Dogwood webinar recording and the monitor's full 2025 report.

Monitor representative Rachel Ryan answered public questions about HCA's ownership of Mission, clarifying that the Asset Purchase Agreement binds HCA not to sell the hospital during the first ten years without advisory board consent and that she has no knowledge of any HCA desire to sell. Ryan restated the 2025 report's two areas of potential noncompliance: service reductions (including discontinued industrial rehabilitation services and reduced pediatric subspecialties) and repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations. The webinar, hosted by Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors on August 4 with more than 90 attendees, reflects ongoing community demand for accountability. The July 2026 monitoring report separately confirmed Mission is not in substantial compliance with the APA, with no staffing plans submitted.

NC Department of Justice July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA Summary Judgment — AG Jackson's Mission Hospital Lawsuit Proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp ruled on July 28, 2026 in favor of AG Jeff Jackson, rejecting HCA's attempt to have the Mission Hospital lawsuit dismissed. The ruling affirms that the purchase agreement requires HCA to actually provide — not merely nominally offer — emergency, trauma, and oncology services, and allows claims related to emergency department staffing and patient care to continue. HCA has denied the allegations.

How to position this
  • A Business Court judge has now ruled that HCA's purchase commitments require actually delivering care — not the hollowed-out version Mission has provided since 2019.
  • The lawsuit proceeds on the core claims: HCA gutted emergency and oncology services at the only hospital serving most of WNC, violating the commitments it made to win regulatory approval.
  • The summary judgment denial coincides with a federal monitor report finding potential noncompliance and no submitted staffing plans — pressure is compounding from multiple directions simultaneously.
  • Advocates: the AG lawsuit, the federal monitor, and CMS oversight are three simultaneous accountability tracks — all pointing to the same conclusion about HCA's failure to honor its obligations.

Judge Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment in the NC Attorney General's lawsuit, ruling that the Asset Purchase Agreement requires HCA to take steps necessary to actually deliver emergency, trauma, and oncology services at Mission — not simply maintain a token presence. Claims related to ED staffing and patient care quality will proceed. The ruling came the same week Dogwood Health Trust's 2025 monitoring report flagged two new areas of potential noncompliance, and one day after the July 26 CMS compliance deadline for Mission's Enhanced Plan of Correction. HCA has denied all allegations and maintains it has met its contractual obligations.
