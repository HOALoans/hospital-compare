Asheville Watchdog / NC Health News August 25, 2026 CON

92 Beds Up for Grabs Amid Public Anger at HCA and Distrust of State Regulators

More than a hundred residents packed a Buncombe County hearing as four hospital systems — Mission, AdventHealth, UNC Health, and Novant — competed for 92 new acute-care beds. Speakers made clear that community fury at HCA and skepticism of the CON process that already handed Mission 95 beds despite active federal safety sanctions has not cooled. A ruling is expected in December 2026.

How to position this
  • State already awarded Mission 95 CON beds amid active CMS Immediate Jeopardy sanctions — granting more beds to an understaffed, noncompliant hospital rewards failure.
  • Mission's own CON application projects bed capacity growing 13% while staffing grows only 3.7% — a staffing-to-beds ratio that would worsen an already dangerous shortfall.
  • Community testimony shows WNC residents do not trust HCA to improve care; three nonprofit competitors are offering alternatives Mission refuses to provide.
  • Advocates should push DHHS to condition any new Mission beds on verified staffing ratios, CMS compliance, and independent monitor sign-off.

A public hearing August 19 at Asheville-Buncombe Technical Community College drew over 100 residents to weigh in on competing CON applications from Mission/HCA, AdventHealth, UNC Health West, and Novant Health for 92 acute-care beds. Community members voiced sharp anger at HCA's stewardship of Mission and deep skepticism of a regulatory process that recently awarded Mission 95 beds while the hospital was under federal safety sanctions. Mission's application proposes adding up to 92 beds to reach 869 total at a cost of $66.8 million. A DHHS decision is expected by December 2026.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly Pricing

Major Investigation: Mission Hospital Charges Double Competing Rates Under HCA Monopoly

A joint Washington Post/KFF Health News investigation by Peter Whoriskey documented how HCA's control of Mission — a "prepackaged monopoly" — drives prices far above regional peers: a knee replacement cost roughly $40,000 at Mission versus about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center. Rand 2024 data put Mission's commercial rates at 334% of Medicare, versus 237% at Catawba and a 280% NC benchmark. The piece features Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Julie Mayfield.

How to position this
  • Mission's commercial rates are 334% of Medicare — 54 percentage points above the NC benchmark — a direct consequence of HCA's monopoly pricing power after the 2019 acquisition.
  • Patients and local employers pay the price: a knee replacement at Mission costs roughly $40,000 versus ~$16,000 at a competitive market peer.
  • HCA's CEO lobbied Congress to eliminate CON laws (April 2026) while wielding monopoly control built on CON-protected market dominance in Asheville.
  • This investigation, featuring Reclaim Healthcare WNC, puts national attention on Asheville as a warning for other communities facing for-profit hospital consolidation.

The August 9 investigation traces how Mission's dominant market position — rooted in a 1998 merger, a 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and HCA's 2019 acquisition — insulates it from price competition across Western NC. Under the same UnitedHealthcare plan, a breast biopsy cost $7,500 at Mission versus $1,700 elsewhere, and a hernia repair $17,700 versus $9,600. Yale economist Zack Cooper and employer Katie Button are featured alongside patient Marcelle Crago and Reclaim Healthcare WNC advocates. The article calls Mission a case study in how hospital monopolies inflate costs nationally.

WLOS August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent Monitor: HCA Cannot Sell Mission Without Advisory Board Consent for 10 Years

Monitor representative Rachel Ryan clarified at a public Q&A that under the Asset Purchase Agreement HCA is barred from selling Mission during the first 10 years of ownership without advisory board consent, and she is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. Ryan also restated the 2025 report's findings of potential noncompliance tied to service discontinuation and multiple Immediate Jeopardy designations.

How to position this
  • The 10-year no-sale provision offers limited community protection — HCA can continue degrading services under the APA while still legally retaining ownership.
  • Monitor's confirmed potential noncompliance on service discontinuation and four IJ citations shows the APA is not self-enforcing; the AG lawsuit and court oversight remain essential.
  • Community members deserve to know: even if HCA wanted to sell, the process requires advisory board consent — a transparency gap the public Q&A helped close.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC should press both the monitor and DHHS to define concrete compliance benchmarks and timelines before any new CON beds are awarded to Mission.

In a public Q&A following the release of the 2025 monitoring report, Affiliated Monitors representative Rachel Ryan addressed community concerns about a possible HCA sale of Mission. She explained the APA prevents a sale during the first 10 years without advisory board consent and said she has no indication HCA intends to sell. Ryan also reaffirmed the monitor's finding of potential noncompliance related to discontinued services and repeated Immediate Jeopardy citations — the clearest public restatement of accountability findings since the July 28 court ruling.

NC Department of Justice / Blue Ridge Public Radio July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA Summary Judgment — AG Jackson's Mission Hospital Lawsuit Proceeds

A NC Business Court judge denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, rejecting the company's attempt to end the AG's case before trial. The ruling allows claims related to emergency department staffing and patient care to proceed, with the court finding sufficient evidence that HCA may have breached its acquisition commitments. HCA denied all allegations; Mission said it is "confident in our compliance" with the terms of the 2019 sale.

How to position this
  • A court found enough evidence of HCA's broken promises to send the case to trial — this is the strongest legal validation yet of community concerns about Mission's care failures.
  • Expert testimony cited by the court found Mission's ED staffing plan incapable of providing adequate care — corroborating what nurses, doctors, and patients have said for years.
  • HCA spent years and millions in legal fees trying to kill this case before trial; the court's denial means the public will finally get to see the evidence.
  • Advocates should note: the AG's office, federal monitor, and independent monitor have all now found evidence of HCA's noncompliance — a convergence of accountability pressure HCA cannot dismiss.

On July 28, 2026, a North Carolina judge rejected HCA's motion for summary judgment in the AG's lawsuit alleging HCA violated commitments made when it purchased Mission Health in 2019. The court found the Attorney General had produced sufficient evidence — including expert testimony that Mission's ED staffing plan could not support adequate care — to send the case forward. The ruling preserves claims on emergency department staffing and patient care; one narrower claim related to ENT coverage was dismissed. HCA continues to deny the allegations. The lawsuit was originally filed in 2023 by then-AG Josh Stein and is now carried forward by AG Jeff Jackson.

HCA Healthcare Investor Relations / SEC July 24, 2026 Earnings

HCA Reports Q2 2026: $20.23B Revenue, $7.62 EPS — Buybacks Surge to $2.06B in Quarter

HCA reported Q2 2026 revenue of $20.23 billion (up 8.7% year-over-year) and net income of $1.70 billion, or $7.62 diluted EPS (up 11.6%). The company repurchased 4.75 million shares for $2.06 billion in the quarter alone, with $7.21 billion remaining under its repurchase authorization — while declaring a $0.78 quarterly dividend. Cash flow from operations fell to $2.34 billion from $4.21 billion a year earlier.

How to position this
  • HCA spent $2.06 billion buying back its own stock in Q2 alone — more than 30 times what Mission's CON application budgets for 92 new beds ($66.8M), yet no staffing plans have been submitted to federal monitors.
  • $7.21 billion remains in HCA's buyback authorization while Mission Hospital has 3.7 FTE per bed — far below the NC average of 5.1 — and the federal monitor confirmed noncompliance in July 2026.
  • HCA's IR press release does not mention the NC AG lawsuit, four Immediate Jeopardy citations, federal monitor noncompliance finding, or Mission's 2-star patient experience ratings.
  • Investors and analysts should demand: what is HCA's financial exposure from the AG lawsuit and potential remediation costs if courts find breach of the Mission acquisition contract?

HCA's official Q2 2026 earnings, reported July 24, showed revenue of $20.23 billion and diluted EPS of $7.62, both beating prior-year figures. The company returned $2.06 billion to shareholders through share repurchases in a single quarter, with $7.21 billion still authorized for future buybacks, and declared a $0.78 per-share quarterly dividend. Same-facility admissions rose 2.5% and ER visits 3.6%, though inpatient and outpatient surgical volumes declined. Total debt stood at $49.72 billion against total assets of $63.25 billion.

HCA Healthcare / SEC 8-K / Healthcare Dive July 14, 2026 Guidance

HCA Cuts Full-Year 2026 Guidance as Payer Mix Shifts Drive Uninsured Volume Higher

HCA lowered its full-year 2026 diluted EPS guidance to $28.70–$30.50 (from $29.10–$31.50) and trimmed revenue guidance to $77.0–$79.5 billion, citing an unfavorable payer mix shift as more patients lose ACA exchange coverage. The company expects up to $1.1 billion in losses from the ACA coverage decline in 2026, partially offset by approximately $400 million in Medicaid Supplemental Payment Program benefits, primarily in Florida.

How to position this
  • HCA's guidance cut exposes the fragility of a for-profit model that depends on insured volume — when coverage erodes, patients suffer and shareholders feel it, but Mission's staffing crisis continues regardless.
  • The same policy environment increasing HCA's uninsured burden is the one HCA's CEO failed to address in April congressional testimony — where he instead lobbied against CON laws that limit Mission's monopoly competition.
  • HCA's $400M Florida Medicaid windfall shows the company's financial resilience rests on state-by-state political access, not operational efficiency or care quality improvements.
  • A guidance cut while carrying $49.72B in debt, spending $2B+ on buybacks, and facing a federal noncompliance finding at Mission raises serious questions about HCA's true cost-of-accountability exposure.

On July 14, 2026, HCA previewed Q2 results and revised full-year 2026 guidance downward, reducing diluted EPS expectations to $28.70–$30.50 and revenue to $77.0–$79.5 billion. The revision was driven primarily by a policy-driven payer mix shift: more patients losing insurance through ACA exchanges are arriving uninsured, a trend HCA projected would cost up to $1.1 billion for 2026. About $400 million in Florida Medicaid supplemental payments partially offset the impact. Shares fell sharply on the news.
