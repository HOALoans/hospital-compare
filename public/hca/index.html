Mission's Legal Campaigns Have Delayed Needed Hospital Beds Across NC for Years

A new investigation finds that Mission/HCA's pattern of appealing competitor CON awards has kept Buncombe County without a hospital rival for years. While AdventHealth Weaverville's 67-bed hospital is finally under construction after surviving three years of HCA legal challenges, Mission's appeal of 26 additional AdventHealth beds has dragged on unresolved for two years. Mission remains the only hospital in Buncombe County, the most populous county in NC's mountain region.

  • More beds for Mission under HCA's ownership means more patients entering a hospital with a 2-star HCAHPS rating and a staffing ratio of 3.7 FTE/bed — far below the NC average of 5.1.

Despite losing three rounds of appeals — including at the NC Supreme Court — over AdventHealth Weaverville's 67-bed facility, Mission is still contesting 26 additional beds awarded to AdventHealth in 2024. Asheville Watchdog August 26, 2026 Safety

Lawsuit Alleges Nurse Anesthetist's Failure to Monitor Led to Patient Death During Routine Mission Hospital Foot Surgery

A new Buncombe County lawsuit alleges that a nurse anesthetist at Mission Hospital failed to respond to steadily declining vital signs during a routine foot surgery, resulting in a patient's fatal heart attack. Noe Serrano Hernandez, 64, was put under anesthesia on September 13, 2024; over 51 minutes his vitals weakened without intervention, and he suffered a cardiac arrest. He never regained the ability to breathe independently and his family withdrew care a week later. The suit names HCA Healthcare and Mission as defendants.

How to position this
  • This lawsuit is one of a pattern: Mission's staffing collapse — from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE per bed — creates the conditions for monitoring failures that can turn routine procedures fatal.
  • HCA earned $20.23 billion in Q2 2026 revenue while Mission continued to operate with a nurse-to-bed ratio far below the NC average; the Serrano Hernandez case illustrates what that gap costs patients.
  • CMS's four Immediate Jeopardy citations at Mission all document the same systemic failures — understaffing, poor monitoring, delayed response — that this lawsuit alleges contributed to a preventable death.
  • Ask HCA: when will Mission's staffing plan be submitted to the federal monitor, and what accountability exists for individual cases of negligent monitoring at an understaffed facility?

A civil lawsuit filed August 21, 2026, in Buncombe County Superior Court alleges that nurse anesthetist Andrew Hunter failed to intervene as 64-year-old Noe Serrano Hernandez's vital signs deteriorated during a routine foot ulcer surgery at Mission Hospital on September 13, 2024. Serrano Hernandez suffered a heart attack, never regained independent breathing, and died after his family withdrew care. The suit names HCA Healthcare and Mission Hospital as defendants, alleging corporate and nursing negligence. The case adds to a growing record of patient deaths tied to staffing failures and monitoring lapses at HCA-owned Mission.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

Investigation: Mission Hospital Charges More Than Double Competitor Prices Under HCA's Asheville Monopoly

A major KFF Health News / Washington Post investigation by Peter Whoriskey documents how HCA's 2019 acquisition of a 'prepackaged monopoly' has driven prices to 334% of Medicare at Mission — nearly 100 points above the NC benchmark — while comparable procedures cost roughly half as much at competing hospitals. A knee replacement runs ~$40,000 at Mission versus ~$16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center; breast biopsies and hernia repairs show similar 2–4x gaps. The investigation traces the problem to Mission's 1998 merger, the 2015 repeal of NC profit limits, and HCA's pricing power after acquiring an already-dominant regional system.

How to position this
  • Mission's commercial prices are 334% of Medicare — nearly 60 points above the NC benchmark of 280% — giving HCA a monopoly pricing premium on top of an already-dominant market position inherited in 2019.
  • WNC employers like Katie Button are trapped paying HCA's inflated rates with no competitive alternatives, a direct consequence of the monopoly HCA acquired and Mission's serial suppression of competitor CON applications.
  • Despite charging monopoly prices, Mission delivers 2-star HCAHPS patient experience ratings every year from 2020 to 2024 — the lowest of all CON applicants in the state.
  • HCA's pricing extraction from WNC patients and employers is the financial engine behind $20+ billion quarterly revenues and $2.1 billion in Q2 share buybacks — capital that could instead fund Mission staffing.

Investigative reporting by Peter Whoriskey for KFF Health News and the Washington Post finds that Mission Hospital's commercial prices reached 334% of Medicare in Rand's 2024 analysis, versus a 237% rate at Catawba Valley Medical Center and an NC benchmark of 280%. Under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement cost roughly $40,000 at Mission and $16,000 at Catawba; similar gaps appear for breast biopsies and hernia repairs. The investigation features WNC employer Katie Button, patient Marcelle Crago, Yale economist Zack Cooper, and Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC. It traces Mission's pricing power to the 1998 regional merger, the 2015 repeal of NC profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of an existing monopoly.

On July 28, 2026, a North Carolina judge rejected HCA's motion for summary judgment in Attorney General Jeff Jackson's lawsuit, which alleges HCA failed to maintain emergency and cancer care at Mission Hospital as promised in the 2019 Asset Purchase Agreement.   • The federal monitor confirmed in July 2026 that Mission has still submitted no staffing plans — Hazen's disclosure of a 'workforce deficit' to investors makes that noncompliance even harder to excuse.
  • Ask HCA's board: if Mission's labor shortage is material enough to flag on an earnings call, why has no staffing plan been submitted to the federal monitor after seven years of HCA ownership?

In an April 25, 2026 earnings call following Q1 results that missed analyst expectations, HCA CEO Sam Hazen told Wall Street that NC hospitals were 'a little behind' profit targets due to elevated labor costs at Mission Hospital, citing a 'significant workforce deficit' in western NC he attributed partly to Hurricane Helene's regional impact. Hazen said HCA was importing contract nursing and non-nursing staff to meet demand.
