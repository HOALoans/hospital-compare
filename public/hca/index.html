WLOS / 828 News NOW September 3, 2026 CON

Mission Hospital Seeks State Approval for 10 More Neonatal Beds in $25.9M Expansion

HCA-owned Mission filed a certificate-of-need application to add 10 neonatal beds at its Asheville campus at a cost of $25.9 million, with a projected completion date of July 2029. A public hearing is scheduled for October 15 in Asheville. The application comes as Mission continues to operate under a history of federal safety sanctions and an active AG lawsuit.

How to position this
  • HCA is seeking to expand its NICU monopoly footprint even as federal safety citations, an AG lawsuit, and a monitor's noncompliance finding remain unresolved.
  • NC DHHS approved Mission's 95-bed CON application in April while active federal sanctions were in place — advocates should demand that safety record be weighed in any new approval.
  • A $25.9M expansion signals HCA's intent to deepen its stranglehold on WNC healthcare while refusing to invest in the staffing ratios or corrective plans regulators have demanded.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and community members have the right to submit public comment before the October 15 CON hearing.

MH Mission Hospital LLLP filed a CON application with NC DHHS to develop 10 new neonatal beds as part of a $25.9 million project expected to be completed in July 2029. A public hearing is set for October 15 at the Pack Memorial Activity Room in Asheville. The move comes as HCA's Mission already won 95 acute-care beds in April 2026 despite four Immediate Jeopardy citations since its 2019 acquisition. Mission is the only Level III NICU provider in Western North Carolina, giving it significant leverage in state CON proceedings.

Asheville Watchdog August 26, 2026 Safety

Wrongful Death Lawsuit: Patient Died After Nurse Anesthetist Failed to Act During Foot Surgery

A new Buncombe County lawsuit alleges that a nurse anesthetist at HCA's Mission Hospital failed to intervene as 64-year-old Noe Serrano Hernandez suffered a fatal heart attack during a routine foot surgery on September 13, 2024. His vital signs deteriorated over 51 minutes without response; he never regained the ability to breathe independently and died a week later. The suit targets HCA Healthcare as Mission's owner.

How to position this
  • This is the latest in a pattern of patient deaths at Mission linked to supervision failures and inadequate staffing — the same deficiencies cited repeatedly by CMS.
  • HCA's staffing ratio fell from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE per bed after the 2019 acquisition; understaffed operating rooms create exactly the conditions alleged in this lawsuit.
  • The AG's ongoing lawsuit also alleges HCA failed to maintain the quality of care it promised — this wrongful death suit provides another on-the-ground example of those failures.
  • CMS found 'systemic and recurring patterns' of harm at Mission — private litigation is now filling the accountability gap that regulators have repeatedly identified but not resolved.

A wrongful death lawsuit filed August 21 in Buncombe County Superior Court by the estate of Noe Serrano Hernandez alleges that a nurse anesthetist monitoring him during a routine foot surgery at Mission Hospital failed to respond to his deteriorating vital signs for 51 minutes, during which he suffered a cardiac arrest. Serrano Hernandez, 64, was put under anesthesia at 8:25 a.m. on September 13, 2024, and never regained the ability to breathe on his own. He died a week later. The suit names HCA Healthcare, Mission's for-profit parent, as a defendant.

A state-required CON public hearing on August 19 at Asheville-Buncombe Technical College drew more than 100 attendees who heard pitches from Mission, AdventHealth, UNC Health West, and Novant Health for 92 new acute-care beds serving Buncombe, Clay, Graham, Madison, and Yancey counties. The hearing was charged with public anger at HCA's record at Mission, with community members and rural officials questioning whether the region's only major hospital system should continue to receive state approvals while under federal oversight. KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

National Investigation: Mission Hospital's Monopoly Pricing — Knee Replacement Costs 2.5× Competing Hospitals

A major KFF Health News investigation by Peter Whoriskey found that Mission Hospital charges dramatically higher prices than non-monopoly hospitals in North Carolina: a knee replacement costs roughly $40,000 at Mission vs. about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center under the same Blue Cross plan. Mission's commercial prices were 334% of Medicare in 2024 — well above the NC benchmark of 280%. The report traces the pricing power to Mission's 1998 merger, HCA's 2019 acquisition of a "prepackaged monopoly," and the 2015 repeal of state profit limits.

How to position this
  • Mission charges patients and insurers 334% of Medicare rates — nearly 60 percentage points above the NC average — directly traceable to HCA's monopoly pricing power after the 2019 acquisition.
  • Employers like Katie Button can't afford to self-insure because Mission controls the market; WNC residents pay the price in premiums, out-of-pocket costs, and delayed care.
  • HCA's CEO told Congress in April 2026 that HCA improves healthcare affordability — the KFF investigation, featuring Mission Hospital specifically, tells a starkly different story.
  • Share this investigation with your legislators, employers, and insurer contacts: the monopoly pricing problem documented here is a direct result of regulatory failures this dashboard tracks.

Investigative reporter Peter Whoriskey found that HCA's Mission Hospital charges roughly $40,000 for a knee replacement compared to about $16,000 at Catawba Valley under the same Blue Cross plan, and that Mission's commercial prices were 334% of Medicare — far above North Carolina's 280% benchmark and Catawba's 237%. The report features Asheville patient Marcelle Crago, restaurant employer Katie Button, Yale economist Zack Cooper, and Sen. Julie Mayfield, who is affiliated with Reclaim Healthcare WNC. It traces Mission's monopoly to its 1998 regional merger and argues that HCA's 2019 purchase compounded an already-dominant market position. A breast biopsy runs $7,500 at Mission vs. $1,700 elsewhere; hernia repair $17,700 vs. $9,600.

NCDOJ / Blue Ridge Public Radio July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

AG Jackson Wins Key Rulings: HCA/Mission Lawsuit Proceeds — Court Rejects HCA's Bid to Avoid Trial

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment and agreed with AG Jeff Jackson that Mission's 2019 asset purchase agreement "requires HCA to take all steps necessary to actually provide" emergency and cancer services — not merely to try. All claims in the case will proceed to trial. The ruling came the same week the independent monitor formally notified the AG's office of two areas of potential noncompliance by HCA.

How to position this
  • A federal judge has now confirmed that HCA's commitments in the 2019 purchase agreement are enforceable — this is a major win that keeps all accountability claims alive.
  • The court rejected HCA's framing that 'reasonable efforts' are sufficient; HCA must actually deliver the emergency and oncology care it promised when acquiring Mission for $1.5 billion.
  • The lawsuit and the monitor's simultaneous noncompliance notification create converging legal and contractual pressure on HCA — this is the strongest accountability moment since the acquisition.
  • Use 'lawsuit' — not 'trial' — when describing the case at this stage; the case is proceeding toward trial but has not yet begun.

In a 55-page ruling issued July 28, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied both HCA's and the AG's cross-motions for summary judgment, meaning all claims in AG Jeff Jackson's lawsuit proceed to trial. The court accepted the AG's argument that the 2019 purchase agreement obligates HCA to actually deliver emergency and cancer care at Mission — not just to make reasonable efforts. Judge Earp also rejected HCA's attempt to exclude testimony from an AG expert witness. The ruling came the same day the independent monitor formally notified the AG's office of two areas of potential noncompliance, including repeated Immediate Jeopardy citations and a disputed service discontinuation.

Arc — running narrative
2019–2026: HCA buys Mission with binding promises → staffing collapse and four Immediate Jeopardy citations → CON beds awarded amid sanctions → AG lawsuit headed to trial → national coverage of monopoly pricing. Read moreShow less
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
Hazen testimony
HCA CEO Sam Hazen testifies before House Ways and Means. Written testimony pushes eliminating CON laws and highlights Helene response in western NC — without addressing Mission’s Immediate Jeopardy citations, staffing collapse, or the AG lawsuit.
Jul 24, 2026
Q2 Earnings
HCA reports strong Q2 2026 results. Mission's noncompliance is not mentioned on the earnings call. "Operational efficiency" language contrasts sharply with the concurrent federal monitor finding of zero staffing plans submitted by Mission.
Jul 26, 2026
Deadline passes
The Enhanced Plan of Correction deadline expires. The federal monitor confirms Mission has not achieved substantial compliance and has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the same deficiencies driving every prior sanction.
Jul 28, 2026
Trial confirmed
Judge denies HCA's summary judgment motion. The NC Attorney General's lawsuit will go to trial. A court will now assess whether HCA honored its legal commitments to WNC. Reclaim's years of documentation, testimony, and public pressure have contributed directly to this moment.
Aug 9, 2026
National spotlight
Washington Post / KFF Health News documents Mission's monopoly pricing: ~$40K vs. ~$16K for the same Blue Cross knee replacement an hour away, and commercial rates at 334% of Medicare. The investigation features Reclaim Healthcare WNC and frames Asheville as a national warning about hospital consolidation.
What's next
Trial date TBD in NC Business Court.
