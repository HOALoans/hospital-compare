KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

Knee surgery costs $40K at Mission, $16K elsewhere — KFF investigation exposes HCA's Asheville monopoly pricing

A major KFF Health News / Washington Post investigation by Peter Whoriskey found that under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement at Mission Hospital cost roughly $40,000 — more than double the ~$16,000 price at Catawba Valley Medical Center. RAND 2024 data place Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare, far above the NC benchmark of 280%. The piece traces Mission's monopoly to a 1998 merger, a 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of a 'prepackaged monopoly.'

How to position this
  • Mission charges more than double the regional rate for common procedures under identical insurance plans — a direct result of the HCA monopoly HCA inherited and has exploited since 2019.
  • RAND data confirm Mission prices at 334% of Medicare, 54 points above the NC benchmark — while staffing ratios have collapsed and patients receive 2-star care.
  • Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC are cited as voices sounding the alarm; this is a national story about Asheville's cautionary tale for communities facing hospital consolidation.
  • HCA marketed its 2019 acquisition as a community benefit; this investigation documents the pricing toll that monopoly ownership has imposed on WNC patients and employers.

A landmark investigative piece by KFF Health News and the Washington Post documents how HCA's Mission Hospital charges dramatically more than regional competitors under the same insurance plans — a knee replacement runs ~$40,000 at Mission versus ~$16,000 at Catawba Valley. UnitedHealthcare data show a breast biopsy at $7,500 vs. $1,700 and hernia repair at $17,700 vs. $9,600. RAND 2024 benchmarks put Mission at 334% of Medicare vs. 237% at Catawba and a 280% NC average. Yale economist Zack Cooper, employer Katie Button, patient Marcelle Crago, and Sen. Julie Mayfield of Reclaim Healthcare WNC all feature in the piece.

WLOS / Dogwood Health Trust August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent monitor: HCA cannot sell Mission in first 10 years without advisory board consent; noncompliance concerns remain

Following the August 4 community Q&A webinar, WLOS reported that independent monitor representative Rachel Ryan confirmed HCA is contractually prohibited from selling Mission Hospital during the first 10 years of ownership without advisory board consent, and that the monitor is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. The monitor also restated the 2025 report's findings of potential noncompliance on service discontinuation and multiple Immediate Jeopardy designations.

How to position this
  • The APA sale-restriction clause provides a key legal protection for WNC — but only if the monitor and AG actively enforce it; Mission's ongoing noncompliance shows enforcement has been inadequate.
  • Potential noncompliance on service discontinuation echoes a pattern: HCA has systematically reduced services it promised to maintain, from oncology to rehabilitation, since 2019.
  • Four Immediate Jeopardy citations in seven years of HCA ownership represent a structural safety failure, not isolated incidents — exactly what the AG lawsuit argues.
  • The community Q&A drew 90+ attendees, reflecting sustained public demand for transparency that HCA and its lobbyists have tried to suppress.

WLOS covered the August 4 community Q&A webinar hosted by Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors, in which independent monitor rep Rachel Ryan addressed public concerns about HCA's ownership. Ryan confirmed that under the Asset Purchase Agreement, HCA is obligated not to sell Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board consent, and said she is unaware of any HCA intention to sell. She reiterated the 2025 monitoring report's findings of potential noncompliance in two areas: service discontinuation (industrial rehabilitation) and Mission's repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations. More than 90 community members attended the webinar.

NC DOJ / Asheville Watchdog July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge denies HCA summary judgment — AG Jackson's Mission Hospital lawsuit proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp largely denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, ruling in favor of several of Attorney General Jeff Jackson's arguments and allowing the lawsuit to advance. The court agreed that the HCA purchase agreement requires HCA to 'take all steps necessary to actually provide' emergency and cancer services at Mission Hospital. AG Jackson stated the case will continue as he fights to defend emergency and cancer care in western North Carolina.

How to position this
  • A North Carolina judge has rejected HCA's attempt to avoid accountability — the legal record now reflects that HCA's obligations under the APA are enforceable, not aspirational.
  • The court's reading that HCA must 'take all steps necessary to actually provide' services is a direct rebuke of HCA's years of minimalist compliance and deflection.
  • The AG lawsuit is the most powerful tool WNC communities have to compel HCA to restore services; this ruling keeps that tool intact and sharpens it.
  • HCA spent years and millions in legal fees trying to kill this case before trial — the denial of summary judgment means the public evidence of care failures will now reach a courtroom.

On July 28, 2026, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp largely denied HCA's motion for summary judgment, ruling that the AG had established viable legal claims that HCA failed to live up to its acquisition commitments on emergency and cancer care. The court agreed the Asset Purchase Agreement requires HCA to actively provide — not merely attempt to provide — those services. AG Jackson framed the ruling as a victory and pledged to keep fighting for WNC residents. The lawsuit, originally filed by then-AG Josh Stein in late 2023, now proceeds toward trial.

Dogwood Health Trust / NC Health News July 28, 2026 Noncompliance

Federal monitor finds HCA in potential noncompliance with Mission sale contract for third straight year

Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors released the 2025 HCA monitoring report on July 28, notifying the NC Attorney General that HCA is in potential noncompliance with the Mission Asset Purchase Agreement in two areas: discontinued rehabilitation services and repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations. The report notes Mission is not in good standing with CMS and is operating under an Enhanced Plan of Correction. It is the third consecutive year the monitor has flagged potential noncompliance.

How to position this
  • Three straight years of potential noncompliance findings establish a documented pattern — this is not a one-time lapse but a systemic failure of the commitments HCA made to earn regulatory approval of the 2019 acquisition.
  • Service discontinuation of the industrial rehab program at CarePartners is exactly the type of community-benefit erosion advocates warned about when HCA bought Mission; the monitor's finding validates those concerns.
  • Mission's Enhanced Plan of Correction status means federal regulators and the independent monitor both agree HCA has not fixed the underlying safety culture problems.
  • Advocates should use this report in every legislative and regulatory forum: the monitor's own findings justify stronger enforcement, new conditions, or divestiture conversations.

The annual independent monitoring report covering calendar year 2025 found two areas of potential noncompliance with HCA's 2019 acquisition commitments: functional discontinuation of the industrial rehabilitation program at CarePartners (caseload dropped to zero by April 2025) and Mission's pattern of Immediate Jeopardy citations. Mission entered the report period under an Enhanced Plan of Correction following repeated failures to satisfy CMS safety requirements. The monitor's letter was transmitted to the NC Attorney General's office. Dogwood and Affiliated Monitors hosted a community Q&A webinar on August 4 with 90+ attendees.

  • This hearing record is public and citable — use it to show that HCA's D.C. narrative and Mission's on-the-ground reality are irreconcilable.

On April 28, 2026, HCA CEO Sam Hazen testified before the House Ways and Means Committee alongside other major health system CEOs in a hearing focused on hospital costs and policies. Hazen's written testimony highlighted HCA's role in responding to Hurricane Helene in western NC, uncompensated care totals, patient safety investments, and a call to eliminate certificate-of-need laws — but did not address Mission's four Immediate Jeopardy citations since 2019, the staffing ratio collapse from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE/bed, the federal monitor's noncompliance findings, or the NC AG lawsuit alleging HCA violated its acquisition commitments. The timing was notable: NC DHHS had recently awarded Mission 95 CON beds despite active federal sanctions.

HCA Healthcare Investor Relations / SEC July 24, 2026 Earnings

HCA Q2 2026: $20.2B revenue, $7.59 EPS beat — but full-year profit guidance cut on Medicaid payer-mix shift

HCA reported Q2 2026 revenue of $20.23 billion (up 8.7% year-over-year) and adjusted EPS of $7.59, beating consensus estimates. However, the company simultaneously cut its full-year 2026 diluted EPS guidance to $28.70–$30.50 (down from $29.10–$31.50) and trimmed Adjusted EBITDA guidance to $15.40B–$16.10B, citing an unfavorable payer-mix shift driven by declining health insurance exchange coverage and Medicaid Supplemental Payment Program changes. HCA repurchased $2.064 billion in shares during Q2 alone and had $7.21 billion remaining under its buyback authorization.

How to position this
  • HCA spent $2+ billion buying back its own stock in a single quarter while Mission Hospital had no staffing plans filed with its federal monitor and remained under an Enhanced Plan of Correction.
  • Record revenue of $20.2B in one quarter — driven in part by Mission's monopoly pricing at 334% of Medicare — yet WNC patients face collapsed staffing ratios and 2-star patient experience scores.
  • The guidance cut was caused by insured patients losing coverage, not by any Mission accountability or safety investment cost — HCA's financial risk model ignores its own care-quality failures.
  • Earnings calls and investor materials contain zero mention of Mission's IJ citations, AG lawsuit, or federal noncompliance — a material omission that advocates and regulators should flag.

HCA Healthcare reported second-quarter 2026 results on July 24, posting revenue of $20.23 billion and net income of $1.699 billion ($7.62 diluted EPS / $7.59 adjusted EPS), surpassing analyst expectations. The strong topline was offset by a guidance revision: full-year diluted EPS cut to $28.70–$30.50 and Adjusted EBITDA trimmed to $15.40B–$16.10B due to a payer-mix deterioration tied to fewer insured exchange patients and changes to Medicaid supplemental programs. During the quarter HCA repurchased 4.752 million shares at a cost of $2.064 billion and declared its $0.78 quarterly dividend. The stock fell roughly 5–14% on the guidance cut despite the Q2 beat.

Insider Monkey / Yahoo Finance July 24, 2026 Guidance

HCA cuts full-year EPS and EBITDA guidance as Medicaid payer-mix shift bites — analysts slash price targets

Following its Q2 preview on July 14 and the full report on July 24, HCA revised its 2026 full-year guidance downward: net income forecast cut to $6.3B–$6.7B (from $6.5B–$7.0B), EPS to $28.70–$30.50, and Adjusted EBITDA to $15.40B–$16.10B.   • Guidance cuts driven by uninsured patient growth expose HCA's vulnerability to the very Medicaid and ACA rollbacks that hurt WNC's most vulnerable patients — patients Mission is failing in quality and safety.
  • Analysts cut targets but maintained buy ratings, reflecting no market penalty for Mission's safety failures — advocates must make the legal and reputational liability visible to institutional investors.
  • When HCA says it is managing 'payer mix headwinds,' WNC advocates should ask: how many of those newly uninsured patients will end up at Mission, where staffing ratios are at crisis levels?

HCA's July 2026 guidance revision represented a meaningful step down from earlier 2026 expectations: EPS guidance fell by roughly $0.80 at the midpoint, EBITDA guidance was trimmed by approximately $250 million at the midpoint, and revenue was narrowed to $77.0B–$79.5B. The culprit was a payer-mix deterioration — more uninsured patients and changes to Medicaid Supplemental Payment Programs — rather than any operational problem at the hospital level. Arc — running narrative
2019–2026: HCA buys Mission with binding promises → staffing collapse and four Immediate Jeopardy citations → CON beds awarded amid sanctions → AG lawsuit headed to trial → national coverage of monopoly pricing. Read moreShow less
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
