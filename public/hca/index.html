Asheville Watchdog September 10, 2026 Congress

Rep. Edwards Introduces 'HCA Mission Act' to Give CMS Power to Fine Hospitals for Repeat Safety Failures

Congressman Chuck Edwards (R-NC-11) introduced the Health Care Accountability of Mission Act of 2026 (H.R. 10311) on September 8, directly in response to Mission Hospital's four Immediate Jeopardy citations since HCA's 2019 acquisition. The bill would allow CMS to fine hospitals $10,000 per day if they receive two IJ citations within a two-year period. It was referred to the House Committee on Ways and Means — the same committee where HCA CEO Sam Hazen testified in April without addressing Mission's IJ citations or federal noncompliance.

How to position this
  • Mission's four IJ citations since 2019 — the most of any hospital in the state — directly motivated this federal legislation; HCA's failure to self-correct has now reached Congress.
  • Current CMS enforcement tools lack financial teeth: Mission can receive IJ citations, submit correction plans, have them rejected, and repeat the cycle with no monetary penalty — exactly what has happened four times.
  • HCA CEO Hazen testified before Ways and Means in April 2026 promoting CON elimination and Helene recovery PR while saying nothing about Mission's IJ record or the federal monitor's noncompliance finding — the bill corrects the gap he refused to address.
  • Advocates should press: will HCA support or oppose H.R. 10311? Silence or opposition is itself an accountability story.

Rep. Chuck Edwards introduced H.R. 10311, the Health Care Accountability of Mission Act of 2026, on September 8, 2026, filed in direct response to Mission Hospital's four Immediate Jeopardy designations since HCA took over in 2019. The bill would amend Title XVIII of the Social Security Act to allow CMS to impose civil monetary penalties of $10,000 per day on hospitals that receive two or more Immediate Jeopardy citations within a two-year period. The bill was referred to the House Committee on Ways and Means. Notably, HCA CEO Sam Hazen testified before that same committee in April 2026 and made no mention of Mission's IJ citations, staffing collapse, or federal noncompliance findings.

Mission Files CON Application for 10 Neonatal Beds — a $25.9 Million Expansion — While Still Under Federal Safety Scrutiny

Mission Hospital filed a Certificate of Need application with NC DHHS to add 10 neonatal beds in a $25.9 million project, with a target completion date of July 2029. A public comment hearing is scheduled for October 15 in Asheville. The filing comes as Mission remains under an Enhanced Plan of Correction from CMS and with four Immediate Jeopardy citations on its record since 2019.

How to position this
  • HCA is aggressively expanding Mission's footprint — 95 beds already awarded in March, now 10 neonatal beds — while the federal monitor says Mission is not in substantial compliance and has submitted no adequate staffing plans.
  • Expanding bed counts without fixing staffing is the wrong direction: Mission's staff ratio fell from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE/bed post-acquisition; more beds without more staff means more risk to patients.
  • NC DHHS has awarded or is considering hundreds of new Mission beds even as CMS repeatedly finds the hospital's patient-safety plans inadequate — advocates should demand DHHS link CON approvals to demonstrated compliance.
  • Public hearing October 15 is an opportunity for community members to testify against further HCA expansion until Mission demonstrates sustained safe staffing.

On September 3, 2026, WLOS reported that Mission Hospital filed a CON application with NC DHHS to develop 10 additional neonatal beds at a projected cost of $25.9 million, to be completed by July 2029 if approved. A public hearing is set for October 15 at Pack Memorial Library in downtown Asheville. The filing arrives as Mission continues to operate under federal safety oversight following four Immediate Jeopardy citations and a federal monitor finding of noncompliance in July 2026. DHHS previously awarded Mission 95 CON beds in March 2026 despite those active federal safety sanctions.

NC Health News / Asheville Watchdog August 25, 2026 CON

92 New WNC Hospital Beds Up for Grabs — and Public Anger at HCA Fills the Room

More than a hundred people packed an August 19 public hearing at A-B Tech as Mission, AdventHealth, Novant, and UNC Health West all competed for state approval to add 92 new acute-care beds in Buncombe County. Community anger at HCA's ownership of Mission was a prominent theme, with many speakers urging DHHS not to award more beds to a hospital still under federal safety sanctions. The DHHS decision will help determine whether WNC gets a genuine alternative to Mission's monopoly.

How to position this
  • A competitive CON award to AdventHealth, Novant, or UNC Health would be the first meaningful structural check on HCA's monopoly in WNC since the 2019 acquisition — community testimony matters.
  • DHHS awarded Mission 95 beds in March despite active federal safety sanctions; awarding another 92 would further entrench a monopoly that independent research shows charges 334% of Medicare rates, roughly 57 percentage points above the NC benchmark.
  • Mission is already seeking still more neonatal beds (September CON filing) while under a federal Enhanced Plan of Correction — the pattern of expansion amid noncompliance demands regulatory scrutiny.
  • Advocates should reference the KFF/WaPo August 9 investigation: monopoly pricing, not quality, is what Mission's market dominance delivers to WNC patients and employers.

On August 19, 2026, NC DHHS held a public hearing at Asheville-Buncombe Technical College on competing CON applications from Mission, AdventHealth, Novant Health, and UNC Health West to add up to 92 acute-care beds serving Buncombe, Clay, Graham, Madison, and Yancey counties. The hearing drew over 100 attendees, many of whom voiced distrust of HCA and Mission's ability to safely operate additional beds. This CON process follows DHHS's March 2026 decision to award Mission 95 beds despite four Immediate Jeopardy citations, a ruling that AdventHealth and UNC Health are already appealing. The state's decision on the 92 beds will be a critical test of whether regulators will prioritize patient safety and competition over HCA's monopoly position.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

Investigation: Mission Hospital Charges More Than Twice the Price for Same Procedures — a Direct Result of HCA's Prepackaged Monopoly

A major KFF Health News / Washington Post investigation by Peter Whoriskey documents how HCA's acquisition of an already-consolidated Mission system has enabled dramatic price disparities: a knee replacement runs roughly $40,000 at Mission versus $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center under the same Blue Cross plan, and RAND 2024 data shows Mission charging 334% of Medicare versus a 280% NC benchmark. Local employer Katie Button and Yale economist Zack Cooper are featured, as is Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC. The piece traces Mission's monopoly to a 1998 merger, a 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of what the reporting calls a 'prepackaged monopoly.'

How to position this
  • Mission charges 334% of Medicare for commercial procedures — 54 points above the NC benchmark of 280% — and nearly 100 points above comparable community hospitals like Catawba Valley (237%); this is the direct cost of unchecked monopoly.
  • Every insurer operating in Asheville must include Mission in its network because it controls 58% of discharges and is the only Level I trauma center — meaning there is no competitive check on its prices.
  • HCA paid $1.5 billion for what this investigation calls a 'prepackaged monopoly' built through a 1998 merger and enabled by the 2015 repeal of NC's state profit limits; regulators let it happen at every step.
  • WNC residents and employers are effectively captive: Katie Button's 100-employee business has been paying Mission's inflated rates for years with no alternative — the AG lawsuit and CON competition are the only structural remedies on the horizon.

On August 9, 2026, KFF Health News and the Washington Post published an in-depth investigation showing that hospital consolidation — particularly HCA's 2019 acquisition of Mission Health — has produced dramatic pricing disparities in Asheville. Under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement cost roughly $40,000 at Mission compared to about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center; a breast biopsy was $7,500 versus $1,700 at a competing system under UnitedHealthcare. RAND 2024 data places Mission's commercial rates at 334% of Medicare, well above the 280% NC average. The investigation features local employer Katie Button, patient Marcelle Crago, Yale economist Zack Cooper, and quotes from Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC.

NC DOJ / BPR July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies Summary Judgment — AG Jackson's Lawsuit Against HCA Over Mission Hospital Proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp issued a 55-page ruling denying summary judgment motions from both HCA and AG Jeff Jackson, allowing the lawsuit to proceed over HCA's alleged failure to honor emergency, trauma, and oncology commitments made when it bought Mission in 2019. The judge also rejected HCA's bid to exclude a key expert witness for the AG's office. AG Jackson stated that HCA had tried every argument to have the case thrown out and failed.

How to position this
  • HCA's attempt to get the case thrown out before trial was rejected on every front — the judge allowed the AG's core claims and expert testimony to stand, a major win for accountability.
  • The lawsuit covers the same service failures the federal monitor flagged in July 2026: noncompliance on service continuity and multiple IJ designations — the legal and regulatory records are now mutually reinforcing.
  • HCA agreed in writing in 2019 not to discontinue emergency, trauma, or oncology services until at least 2029; the AG's office says those commitments have been broken — Mission's patients bore the cost of that breach.
  • With the lawsuit advancing, HCA faces simultaneous accountability on four tracks: AG lawsuit, federal monitor noncompliance, CMS Enhanced Plan of Correction, and now congressional legislation (H.R. 10311).

On July 28, 2026, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp issued a 55-page ruling denying summary judgment motions from both HCA Healthcare and Attorney General Jeff Jackson, allowing the AG's lawsuit against HCA over its operation of Mission Hospital to move forward. The AG alleges HCA violated its 2019 Asset Purchase Agreement commitments to maintain emergency, trauma, and oncology services. The judge also rejected HCA's attempt to exclude testimony from the AG's expert witness. AG Jackson publicly stated that HCA 'threw every argument against the wall' to get the case dismissed and failed — the lawsuit now advances toward trial.

HCA reported Q2 2026 revenues of $20.23 billion (up from $18.6B in Q2 2025) and net income of $1.699 billion ($7.62 diluted EPS), while buying back approximately $2.1 billion in shares and declaring a $0.78 quarterly dividend payable September 30. The results reflect roughly $400 million in incremental Medicaid supplemental payments, primarily from Florida, which partially offset an unfavorable shift in payer mix toward uninsured patients. HCA's full-year 2026 guidance was revised down from prior targets following the payer mix headwinds disclosed on July 14.

  • $20.23B in quarterly revenue from a system that, in Asheville, charges 334% of Medicare rates (per RAND 2024) — the earnings engine depends in part on the same monopoly pricing that has harmed WNC patients and employers.

HCA Healthcare's Q2 2026 results showed revenues of $20.23 billion (up 8.7% year-over-year), net income of $1.699 billion, and diluted EPS of $7.62 ($7.59 adjusted). Healthcare Dive July 14, 2026 Guidance

HCA Cuts 2026 Profit Forecast After Payer Mix Shift Costs ~$400M — Stock Drops ~7%, Securities Fraud Probe Launched

HCA slashed its 2026 full-year earnings guidance on July 14, citing an unfavorable shift toward uninsured patients following turbulence on the ACA exchanges, which reduced pre-tax income by approximately $400 million in Q2. The company now projects net income of $6.3–$6.7 billion, down from prior guidance of $6.5–$7.0 billion. HCA stock fell roughly 6–7% on the news, and law firms Pomerantz LLP and Kessler Topaz subsequently announced securities fraud investigations into whether HCA and its executives properly disclosed the emerging payer-mix risk to investors.

How to position this
  • The guidance cut exposes a structural vulnerability in HCA's business model: when public insurance coverage erodes (as ACA exchange enrollment falls), HCA hospitals — including Mission — are left treating more uninsured patients who cannot pay full freight, squeezing margins.
  • Securities fraud investigations from multiple law firms suggest investors may not have been fully informed about the payer-mix risk — the same IR communications that omit Mission's IJ citations and AG lawsuit are now under shareholder scrutiny.
  • HCA's response to margin pressure historically has been to cut staffing and services — Mission's post-2019 trajectory (800+ clinical departures, ratio drop from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE/bed) is the playbook that may accelerate under earnings pressure.
  • Advocates should note: HCA's 'disappointing' quarter still produced $20.23B in revenue and $1.7B in net income — the hardship framing should not obscure the scale of capital being extracted from communities like Asheville.

On July 14, 2026, HCA Healthcare issued preliminary Q2 results alongside sharply reduced full-year 2026 earnings guidance, cutting projected net income to $6.3–$6.7 billion from $6.5–$7.0 billion. The guidance cut was driven by an unfavorable payer mix shift — more uninsured patients — tied to ACA exchange disruptions, which cost approximately $400 million in Q2 pre-tax income. HCA's stock fell approximately 6–7% on the announcement. Law firms Pomerantz LLP and Kessler Topaz Meltzer & Check subsequently launched securities fraud investigations into whether HCA disclosed the payer-mix risk to investors in a timely and complete manner.

Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.

The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.

Fourth IJ
