Today's news — Aug 1, 2026
NC Business Court July 28, 2026 Trial

AG Jackson wins key pre-trial rulings — HCA/Mission case proceeding to trial

Judge denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on the core claims that HCA violated its commitments to the WNC community when it purchased Mission in 2019. The state's lawsuit alleges HCA broke promises on staffing, services, and capital investment. A full trial is now on the docket — the most significant legal development in the accountability effort to date.

NC Health News / Dogwood July 28, 2026 Noncompliance

CMS monitor report: Mission still out of compliance — no plans to add staff submitted

The court-appointed federal monitor's latest report found Mission Hospital still failing to meet Enhanced Plan of Correction requirements. Critically, the hospital has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the core deficiencies that triggered every Immediate Jeopardy finding. The July 26 CMS deadline came and went without Mission achieving substantial compliance.

HCA Healthcare July 24, 2026 Earnings

HCA reports strong Q2 2026 results — Mission safety failures invisible in earnings call

HCA posted robust Q2 revenue and earnings, cementing its Fortune 100 status. CEO and CFO language emphasized "cost discipline," "operational efficiency," and margin expansion. Mission Hospital's repeated safety failures, noncompliance, and pending trial were not mentioned. HCA's ability to report record profits while a hospital in its portfolio faces federal sanctions and a state attorney general lawsuit is the central tension Reclaim must amplify.

BPR / NC DHHS April 1, 2026 CON

State awards Mission 95 CON beds — despite active federal safety sanctions

NC DHHS approved HCA/Mission's application for 95 new hospital beds while the hospital was under active federal compliance orders. Reclaim's data shows Mission scored the lowest HCAHPS patient experience rating (2 stars) of all four CON applicants — far behind Advent Health (4 stars), Novant, and UNC Pardee. Reclaim formally opposed the award and testified at the CON hearing.

Talking points for media and advocacy
1
Profits over patients — in HCA's own words. HCA reported record Q2 2026 profits on its earnings call while simultaneously failing to submit any plan to add nurses, trauma staff, or security at Mission — the same deficiencies that triggered four Immediate Jeopardy citations. Ask publicly: if HCA can reward shareholders, why can't it staff Mission safely?
2
The AG's trial is the accountability moment WNC has waited for. The July 28 ruling means a court will hear evidence that HCA broke its 2019 acquisition promises on staffing, services, and investment. This is no longer just a regulatory story — it's a breach-of-contract story with WNC's health on the line.
3
"No plans to add staff" is the headline. The federal monitor's July 2026 report found Mission submitted zero staffing plans. After four Immediate Jeopardy findings, the hospital's own compliance filing documents no commitment to fix the root cause. Every HCA "we are working on it" statement must be measured against this documented inaction.
4
The CON award was a gift to a noncompliant operator. NC DHHS awarded 95 new beds to the hospital system with the worst patient experience scores in the CON competition — while it was under active federal safety orders. More beds, same understaffed hospital: that is a regulatory failure that compounds patient risk.
5
The staffing math is damning. Mission runs 3.7 FTE per patient bed. The NC average is 5.1. That 1.4 FTE-per-bed gap is not a rounding error — it is the structural cause of every Immediate Jeopardy, every adverse patient event, every nurse who has left since 2019.
6
Two stars for five years running. Mission's HCAHPS patient experience rating has been 2 out of 5 stars every year from 2020 through 2024 — consistently the lowest in the WNC CON field. That is not bad luck. That is a management strategy that treats patient experience as a cost to be minimized.
Arc — running narrative
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Jul 24, 2026
Q2 Earnings
HCA reports strong Q2 2026 results. Mission's noncompliance is not mentioned on the earnings call. "Operational efficiency" language contrasts sharply with the concurrent federal monitor finding of zero staffing plans submitted by Mission.
Jul 26, 2026
Deadline passes
The Enhanced Plan of Correction deadline expires. The federal monitor confirms Mission has not achieved substantial compliance and has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the same deficiencies driving every prior sanction.
Jul 28, 2026
Trial confirmed
Judge denies HCA's summary judgment motion. The NC Attorney General's lawsuit will go to trial. A court will now assess whether HCA honored its legal commitments to WNC. Reclaim's years of documentation, testimony, and public pressure have contributed directly to this moment.
What's next
Trial date TBD in NC Business Court. CMS must determine next steps following Mission's failure to achieve substantial compliance — options include continued monitoring, Medicare/Medicaid payment suspension, or termination. HCA Q3 earnings (expected October 2026) will again test whether HCA addresses Mission publicly in investor communications.
