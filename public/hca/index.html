Asheville Watchdog August 26, 2026 Safety

Lawsuit Alleges Nurse Negligence Killed Patient During Routine Foot Surgery at Mission Hospital

A new wrongful-death lawsuit filed August 21 in Buncombe County Superior Court alleges a Mission Hospital nurse anesthetist failed to monitor a 64-year-old patient for 51 minutes during foot surgery, leading to cardiac arrest, brain injury, and death. The suit names HCA Healthcare directly. It is the latest in a string of patient-harm cases tied to Mission's staffing and oversight failures since HCA's 2019 acquisition.

How to position this
  • A patient's death during a routine surgery — 51 minutes without intervention — is a direct consequence of the staffing and oversight collapse HCA has permitted since 2019.
  • The suit names HCA Healthcare as a direct defendant, underscoring that corporate decisions — not individual accidents — are the root cause.
  • Mission's 4 Immediate Jeopardy citations and 2-star HCAHPS ratings are not abstract metrics; they correspond to real patients dying preventably.
  • HCA's Q2 2026 earnings call did not mention this case or Mission's safety record — ask why investor calls are silent while patients bear the consequences.

On September 13, 2024, Noe Serrano Hernandez underwent routine foot surgery at Mission Hospital. According to the lawsuit, nurse anesthetist Andrew Hunter failed to intervene as the patient's vital signs deteriorated over 51 minutes; Serrano Hernandez went into cardiac arrest, suffered a brain injury, and died on September 21 after his family withdrew ventilator support. The lawsuit, filed August 21, 2026 in Buncombe County Superior Court, names HCA Healthcare and Mission directly. It is the latest wrongful-death case alleging that chronic understaffing and inadequate supervision at Mission are producing preventable patient deaths.

NC Health News / Asheville Watchdog August 25, 2026 CON

Community Anger at HCA Fills CON Hearing as Four Systems Fight for 92 Buncombe Beds

More than 100 people packed an August 19 public hearing at Asheville-Buncombe Technical College to comment on competing proposals from Mission Hospital, AdventHealth, UNC Health West, and Novant Health for 92 new acute-care beds in Buncombe County. Public anger at HCA's management of Mission was a dominant theme. NC DHHS already awarded Mission 95 beds in a prior round despite active federal safety sanctions — a decision that drew outcry from competitors and community members alike.

How to position this
  • DHHS awarded Mission 95 CON beds while the hospital was under active federal safety sanctions — regulators must not repeat that mistake with 92 more beds.
  • Community members at the August 19 hearing made clear that Mission's monopoly has failed WNC — the CON process is an opportunity to break that grip.
  • Mission has the lowest HCAHPS scores of all CON applicants and a staffing ratio (3.7 FTE/bed) far below the NC average (5.1) — it should not be rewarded with more beds.
  • Adding beds without fixing staff ratios or safety culture only scales up an unsafe system — DHHS must condition any award on demonstrated, sustained compliance.

At a state Certificate of Need hearing on August 19, 2026, four health systems — Mission Hospital, AdventHealth, UNC Health West, and Novant Health — each presented their case for up to 92 new beds in Buncombe County. Hundreds of community members attended, with many using public comment to voice frustration with HCA's performance at Mission. The stakes are high: Mission already holds a CON for 95 beds awarded earlier in 2026 despite multiple federal safety sanctions, and competitors have appealed that decision. DHHS is expected to rule on the competing 92-bed proposals after the hearing record closes.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

Investigation: Mission Hospital's Monopoly Drives Knee Replacement to $40K — More Than Double a Comparable NC Hospital

A major Washington Post / KFF Health News investigation found that under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement cost roughly $40,000 at Mission Hospital versus about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center. RAND 2024 data shows Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare rates, compared to a 280% NC benchmark. The report traces Mission's pricing power to its 1998 monopoly merger, the 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of a 'prepackaged monopoly.'

How to position this
  • Mission's monopoly pricing — 334% of Medicare, more than double comparable NC hospitals for the same procedure — is a direct tax on every employer and family in WNC.
  • HCA bought a 'prepackaged monopoly' in 2019 and has used that leverage to raise prices while cutting staff and quality; the two harms are inseparable.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Mayfield are named in the investigation — WNC advocates are already on the record connecting monopoly pricing to Mission's accountability failures.
  • Use this story alongside the AG lawsuit: HCA's pricing power is exactly what the acquisition commitments were meant to check — and what the court will now evaluate.

Investigative reporter Peter Whoriskey found stark price disparities tied to Mission's monopoly position in western NC: a knee replacement under the same Blue Cross plan ran ~$40,000 at Mission vs. ~$16,000 at Catawba Valley; a breast biopsy $7,500 vs. $1,700 under UnitedHealthcare; a hernia repair $17,700 vs. $9,600. RAND 2024 data placed Mission's commercial rates at 334% of Medicare vs. a 237% Catawba benchmark and 280% NC average. Yale economist Zack Cooper, employer Katie Button, patient Marcelle Crago, and Sen. Julie Mayfield / Reclaim Healthcare WNC are featured. The report links Mission's leverage to the 1998 regional hospital merger, the 2015 repeal of state price limits, and HCA's acquisition of a ready-made monopoly in 2019.

NC DOJ July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA's Motion to Dismiss AG Lawsuit; Emergency Staffing, Cancer Care Claims Proceed to Trial

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied most of HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, 2026, ruling that Attorney General Jeff Jackson's lawsuit over Mission Hospital's failure to provide adequate emergency and cancer care will proceed. The court found that HCA's purchase agreement 'requires HCA to take all steps necessary to actually provide' those services — and specifically refused to dismiss claims that HCA understaffed Mission and created unsafe nurse-to-patient ratios. The court also rejected HCA's bid to exclude the AG's expert witness.

How to position this
  • A judge has now ruled HCA cannot escape accountability through procedural motions — the facts of Mission's staffing collapse and service failures will be tested at trial.
  • The court's finding that HCA must 'actually provide' emergency and cancer services — not just maintain them on paper — is a landmark interpretation of the 2019 acquisition commitments.
  • Claims about unsafe nurse-to-patient ratios survived summary judgment; Mission's FTE/bed ratio of 3.7 vs. the NC average of 5.1 will be central evidence.
  • Use this ruling in every community conversation: the AG lawsuit is advancing, and HCA is running out of legal options to avoid accountability in WNC.

In a 55-page ruling issued July 28, 2026, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment and rejected HCA's effort to exclude the AG's expert witness, Dr. Kia Parsi. The court agreed with AG Jackson that HCA's acquisition agreement requires it to actually provide — not merely offer — emergency and cancer services, and kept alive the AG's claims that HCA created unsafe nurse-to-patient ratios. The lawsuit, initiated under AG Josh Stein and continued under AG Jeff Jackson, now moves toward trial. Jackson stated publicly that HCA 'threw every argument against the wall' and failed.

NNU Case Study: HCA's National Playbook — Monopoly, Price Hikes, Service Cuts, and How Mission Fits the Pattern
