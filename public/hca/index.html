Mission Hospital Files CON Application for 10 More Neonatal Beds — Amid Ongoing Safety Record

Mission Hospital has filed a certificate of need application with NC DHHS to add 10 neonatal intensive-care beds at a projected cost of $25.9 million, with a target completion of July 2029. The application arrives while Mission still carries the weight of four Immediate Jeopardy citations since HCA's 2019 acquisition and community anger over the state's March 2026 award of 95 beds to Mission despite active safety sanctions. Critics note the pattern of HCA seeking capacity expansion while accountability for care quality remains unresolved.

How to position this
  • HCA is pursuing NICU expansion while Mission carries 4 Immediate Jeopardy citations and a federal noncompliance finding from July 2026 — the state should not reward capacity growth before safety is fixed.
  • DHHS already awarded Mission 95 beds over community objection in March 2026; granting more beds while the AG lawsuit and monitor noncompliance findings are active deepens the accountability gap.
  • Community fury at the August 19 CON hearing showed WNC residents do not trust HCA/Mission to manage expanded capacity responsibly.
  • Ask DHHS: what staffing plan will Mission submit for 10 new NICU beds when it still has not filed required staffing plans under its federal Enhanced Plan of Correction?

Mission Hospital filed a CON application with NC DHHS on or before September 3, 2026, seeking approval to add 10 neonatal beds at a cost of $25.9 million, with completion projected for July 2029. The filing comes as the community is still absorbing DHHS's controversial March 2026 award of 95 acute-care beds to Mission despite repeated federal safety sanctions. A simultaneous CON process for 92 additional Buncombe County beds — drawing Mission, Novant, UNC Health West, and AdventHealth — is underway, with a packed public hearing held August 19, 2026. Reclaim Healthcare WNC and other advocates argue HCA should fix care quality before adding any capacity.

Asheville Watchdog / NC Health News August 9, 2026 Monopoly

WaPo/KFF Investigation: Mission's Monopoly Means Knee Surgery Costs $40K — More Than Double Nearby Competitors

A major investigation by Peter Whoriskey for KFF Health News (published jointly with the Washington Post) documents how Mission Hospital's Buncombe County monopoly — rooted in a 1998 merger and sharpened by HCA's 2019 acquisition — drives prices that are more than twice those of comparable NC hospitals. Under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement at Mission ran roughly $40,000 vs. approximately $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center; Rand 2024 data put Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare vs. a 237% NC-peer benchmark of 280%. Yale economist Zack Cooper and Sen. Julie Mayfield of Reclaim Healthcare WNC are quoted.

How to position this
  • Mission's commercial prices are 334% of Medicare — 57 percentage points above the NC peer benchmark — a direct consequence of HCA buying a prepackaged monopoly in 2019.
  • Knee replacement at Mission costs roughly $40,000 vs. $16,000 at Catawba Valley under the same insurer — WNC patients and employers are paying a monopoly tax.
  • HCA CEO Sam Hazen told Congress in April 2026 that HCA makes care more affordable; KFF/WaPo data show Mission's prices are among the highest in NC.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Julie Mayfield are named in the story — this investigation is a primary resource for advocates pushing for pricing accountability and antitrust reform.

Reporter Peter Whoriskey's August 9, 2026, investigation for KFF Health News — co-published with the Washington Post — details how Mission Hospital's decades-old monopoly position has driven commercial prices dramatically higher than comparable NC hospitals. Rand 2024 data put Mission commercial prices at 334% of Medicare, vs. 237% at Catawba Valley and an NC benchmark of 280%. Patient Marcelle Crago and restaurant employer Katie Button illustrate the real-world costs; Yale economist Zack Cooper explains the mechanism; and Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC provide the accountability frame. The piece traces Mission's pricing power from the 1998 merger through HCA's 2019 purchase of what it calls a 'prepackaged monopoly.'

The webinar drew more than 90 attendees.

NC DOJ / WLOS July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

AG Jackson Wins Key Arguments — HCA/Mission Lawsuit Proceeds After Court Denies Summary Judgment

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied most of HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, 2026, ruling that all claims in AG Jeff Jackson's lawsuit against HCA will proceed. The court agreed with Jackson that HCA's 2019 purchase agreement 'requires HCA to take all steps necessary to actually provide' emergency and cancer services at Mission Hospital. The ruling means WNC communities will get their day in court over HCA's failure to honor its acquisition commitments.

How to position this
  • The court agreed with the AG: HCA's APA requires it to actually provide emergency and cancer care — not just maintain token services — and that claim now goes to trial.
  • HCA's attempt to end the case early failed; the community's legal accountability path remains open and every new IJ citation and monitor noncompliance finding adds to the evidentiary record.
  • Dogwood's simultaneous noncompliance notification to the AG (July 28) creates a two-front accountability pressure: federal monitor noncompliance + active state lawsuit.
  • Reclaim advocates should emphasize: the lawsuit is about broken promises, not just regulatory violations — HCA signed an APA and has not honored it.

On July 28, 2026, North Carolina Business Court Judge Julianna Earp ruled in favor of several of Attorney General Jeff Jackson's arguments in his lawsuit against HCA Healthcare over conditions at Mission Hospital, denying most of HCA's motion for summary judgment. All claims in the case will proceed. The court agreed that the HCA purchase agreement requires HCA to actually provide — not merely make available — emergency and cancer services at Mission. The ruling came the same day Dogwood Health Trust formally notified the AG's office of HCA's potential noncompliance with the Mission sale contract, citing service discontinuation and repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations.

Arc — running narrative
2019–2026: HCA buys Mission with binding promises → staffing collapse and four Immediate Jeopardy citations → CON beds awarded amid sanctions → AG lawsuit headed to trial → national coverage of monopoly pricing. Read moreShow less
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
Hazen testimony
HCA CEO Sam Hazen testifies before House Ways and Means. Written testimony pushes eliminating CON laws and highlights Helene response in western NC — without addressing Mission’s Immediate Jeopardy citations, staffing collapse, or the AG lawsuit.
Jul 24, 2026
Q2 Earnings
HCA reports strong Q2 2026 results. Mission's noncompliance is not mentioned on the earnings call. "Operational efficiency" language contrasts sharply with the concurrent federal monitor finding of zero staffing plans submitted by Mission.
Jul 26, 2026
Deadline passes
The Enhanced Plan of Correction deadline expires. The federal monitor confirms Mission has not achieved substantial compliance and has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the same deficiencies driving every prior sanction.
Jul 28, 2026
Trial confirmed
Judge denies HCA's summary judgment motion. The NC Attorney General's lawsuit will go to trial. A court will now assess whether HCA honored its legal commitments to WNC. Reclaim's years of documentation, testimony, and public pressure have contributed directly to this moment.
