Asheville Watchdog August 26, 2026 Safety

Lawsuit Alleges Patient Died After Nurse Anesthetist Failed to Intervene During Routine Surgery at Mission

A new Buncombe County lawsuit claims that 64-year-old Noe Serrano Hernandez suffered a fatal heart attack during a routine foot surgery on Sept. 13, 2024, after his nurse anesthetist failed to intervene as his vital signs deteriorated for 51 minutes. He was removed from life support a week later. The suit adds to a pattern of safety failures documented by CMS and the independent monitor at HCA-owned Mission Hospital.

How to position this
  • A preventable patient death alleged during routine foot surgery reinforces the real human cost of Mission's documented staffing and supervision failures.
  • Mission has received four CMS Immediate Jeopardy citations since HCA acquired it in 2019 — this lawsuit is another data point in that pattern.
  • HCA's own CEO acknowledged a 'significant workforce deficit' at Mission; this case asks who is supervising patients when staff is stretched thin.
  • Ask HCA: when will staffing ratios at Mission return to safe levels — and who is accountable when they don't?

A lawsuit filed in Buncombe County alleges that Noe Serrano Hernandez, 64, was placed under anesthesia for a routine foot surgery at Mission Hospital on Sept. 13, 2024, and suffered a fatal heart attack after his supervising nurse anesthetist failed to intervene as his vital signs steadily weakened over 51 minutes. Serrano Hernandez never regained the ability to breathe on his own, and his family disconnected life support a week later. The lawsuit accuses Mission Hospital of negligence. The case comes amid Mission's history of four CMS Immediate Jeopardy citations since HCA's 2019 acquisition.

92 WNC Hospital Beds Up for Grabs — Amid Public Anger at HCA and Distrust of State Regulators

More than 100 residents packed a public hearing at A-B Tech on August 19 as Mission, AdventHealth, UNC Health West, and Novant all competed for 92 state-allocated Buncombe County beds. The hearing was charged with frustration at HCA's safety record: Mission already received 95 CON beds in March despite active federal sanctions, a decision still drawing fire. A final ruling is expected in December.

How to position this
  • DHHS awarded Mission 95 CON beds in March while the hospital was under active federal safety sanctions — now HCA is seeking even more.
  • Residents packed the August 19 hearing to voice frustration: more beds for Mission means more market power for a monopoly with a documented safety crisis.
  • HCA CEO Sam Hazen urged Congress on April 28 to eliminate CON laws — while HCA was simultaneously fighting to secure CON beds for Mission, blocking competitors.
  • The CON process is the public's best remaining tool to force accountability: DHHS should weigh Mission's four Immediate Jeopardy citations, 2-star HCAHPS ratings, and noncompliance findings before awarding more beds.

More than 100 residents attended an August 19 NC DHHS public hearing at A-B Tech's Ferguson Auditorium where Mission Hospital, AdventHealth, UNC Health West, and Novant Health all competed for 92 additional acute-care hospital beds in Buncombe County. Mission is currently licensed for 853 beds after receiving 95 CON beds in March even while under federal safety scrutiny. The hearing drew anger from residents and advocates over HCA's track record; a CON decision is expected in December 2026.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

Investigation: Mission's Hospital Monopoly Charges $40K for Knee Surgery That Costs $16K Elsewhere in NC

A major Washington Post/KFF investigation by Peter Whoriskey found Mission charges roughly $40,000 for a knee replacement under the same Blue Cross plan where Catawba Valley Medical Center charges about $16,000. RAND 2024 data place Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare versus 237% at Catawba and 280% for NC overall. The piece traces the problem to the 1998 Mission merger creating a monopoly, 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase of a 'prepackaged monopoly.'

How to position this
  • Mission charges more than double the NC benchmark for common procedures — proof that HCA's monopoly extracts wealth from WNC patients and employers.
  • A breast biopsy costs $7,500 at Mission vs. $1,700 at a comparable facility; a hernia repair $17,700 vs. $9,600 — these are not market rates, they are monopoly rents.
  • HCA's 2019 purchase was described in the investigation as acquiring a 'prepackaged monopoly' — the state and federal government enabled this, and must now act to correct it.
  • Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC are named in national coverage as the accountability voices — use this story in every legislative and advocacy conversation.

Investigative journalist Peter Whoriskey, writing for KFF Health News and The Washington Post, documented how Mission Hospital's monopoly market position enables dramatically higher prices: a knee replacement runs ~$40,000 at Mission versus ~$16,000 at Catawba Valley under the same Blue Cross plan. RAND 2024 data show Mission's commercial rates at 334% of Medicare, well above the 280% NC benchmark and the 237% at Catawba. The piece features Asheville patient Marcelle Crago, employer Katie Button, Yale economist Zack Cooper, and Sen. Julie Mayfield of Reclaim Healthcare WNC, and traces the monopoly to the 1998 Mission merger, 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and HCA's 2019 purchase.

Independent Monitor: HCA Cannot Sell Mission Without Advisory Board Consent — Flags Service Cuts and Repeated IJ Citations as Noncompliance Risks

Monitor rep Rachel Ryan told the public Q&A that under the Asset Purchase Agreement, HCA is prohibited from selling Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board consent, and she is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. The monitor restated the 2025 report's findings of potential noncompliance on service discontinuation and Mission's string of Immediate Jeopardy designations. The webinar, hosted by Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors on August 4, drew 90+ attendees.

  • Potential noncompliance on service discontinuation means WNC patients may already be losing promised care — not just facing poor quality.
  • HCA cannot unilaterally sell Mission for the first 10 years; the community has leverage — and must use the monitor process and AG lawsuit to enforce the APA.
  • Public demand that HCA submit required staffing compliance plans — which the monitor confirmed had not been received as of July 2026.

Arc — running narrative
2019–2026: HCA buys Mission with binding promises → staffing collapse and four Immediate Jeopardy citations → CON beds awarded amid sanctions → AG lawsuit headed to trial → national coverage of monopoly pricing. Read moreShow less
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
Hazen testimony
HCA CEO Sam Hazen testifies before House Ways and Means. Written testimony pushes eliminating CON laws and highlights Helene response in western NC — without addressing Mission’s Immediate Jeopardy citations, staffing collapse, or the AG lawsuit.
Jul 24, 2026
Q2 Earnings
HCA reports strong Q2 2026 results. Mission's noncompliance is not mentioned on the earnings call. "Operational efficiency" language contrasts sharply with the concurrent federal monitor finding of zero staffing plans submitted by Mission.
Jul 26, 2026
Deadline passes
The Enhanced Plan of Correction deadline expires. The federal monitor confirms Mission has not achieved substantial compliance and has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the same deficiencies driving every prior sanction.
Jul 28, 2026
Trial confirmed
Judge denies HCA's summary judgment motion. The NC Attorney General's lawsuit will go to trial. A court will now assess whether HCA honored its legal commitments to WNC. Reclaim's years of documentation, testimony, and public pressure have contributed directly to this moment.
Aug 9, 2026

Washington Post / KFF Health News documents Mission's monopoly pricing: ~$40K vs. ~$16K for the same Blue Cross knee replacement an hour away, and commercial rates at 334% of Medicare.
