KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

National investigation: Mission's monopoly pricing charges patients more than double for the same procedures

A major KFF Health News / Washington Post investigation by Peter Whoriskey documents how Mission Hospital's monopoly position drives prices far above regional peers — a knee replacement costs roughly $40,000 at Mission versus about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center under the same Blue Cross plan. Mission's commercial prices run at 334% of Medicare rates, versus a 280% NC benchmark and 237% at Catawba. The piece traces the roots of HCA's 'prepackaged monopoly' to the 1998 Mission merger and 2015 repeal of state profit limits, and features Sen. Julie Mayfield and Reclaim Healthcare WNC.

How to position this
  • Mission charges more than double for common procedures compared to nearby hospitals — the direct cost of HCA's unchecked monopoly in WNC.
  • Mission's commercial prices hit 334% of Medicare rates, 54 points above NC's own benchmark and nearly 100 points above Catawba Valley.
  • HCA acquired a 'prepackaged monopoly' in 2019 and has used that market power to raise prices while cutting staff and quality — a combination that harms patients twice.
  • Sen. Julie Mayfield: 'Unregulated monopolies have never gone well for the public.' Asheville is the cautionary tale for every community facing a hospital merger.

An in-depth investigation by KFF Health News and the Washington Post exposes how Mission Hospital, now owned by HCA Healthcare, charges dramatically higher prices than regional competitors due to its monopoly position in western NC. Under the same UnitedHealthcare plan, a breast biopsy cost $7,500 at Mission versus $1,700 elsewhere, and a hernia repair $17,700 versus $9,600. Rand 2024 data places Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare — well above the NC benchmark of 280%. Yale economist Zack Cooper, employer Katie Button, patient Marcelle Crago, and Sen. Julie Mayfield of Reclaim Healthcare WNC are all featured.

WLOS August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent monitor: HCA cannot sell Mission without advisory board consent; noncompliance findings stand

Following the August 4 community Q&A webinar, WLOS reported that independent monitor representative Rachel Ryan clarified that under the Asset Purchase Agreement, HCA is contractually obligated not to sell Mission Hospital during the first 10 years without advisory board consent — and that the monitor is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. Ryan restated the 2025 report's two areas of potential noncompliance: discontinuation of services at CarePartners/Mission and the hospital's repeated Immediate Jeopardy citations in 2025 and 2026.

How to position this
  • The APA's anti-sale clause is a key protection for WNC — the community must demand it be enforced, not quietly waived.
  • Two formal potential-noncompliance findings — on service cuts and on repeated Immediate Jeopardy — remain unresolved and must be answered before any compliance claim by HCA is accepted.
  • Repeated IJ citations (2021, 2024, 2025, 2026) are explicitly flagged by the monitor as a potential APA violation — HCA cannot paper over a pattern of endangering patients.
  • 90+ community members attended the public webinar, reflecting sustained WNC demand for transparency that HCA's own communications have failed to provide.

WLOS covered the independent monitor's August 4 public Q&A webinar, at which monitor general counsel Rachel Ryan addressed community questions about HCA's long-term ownership of Mission. She confirmed that the APA prohibits HCA from selling Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board approval, and said the monitor is unaware of any HCA intent to sell. Ryan also reaffirmed that the 2025 monitoring report identified two areas of potential noncompliance: service diminishment at CarePartners and Mission, and the hospital's repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations in 2025 and 2026.

NC Department of Justice / BPR July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge denies HCA summary judgment motions — AG Jackson's Mission Hospital lawsuit proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp issued a 55-page ruling on July 28, denying both HCA's and AG Jeff Jackson's motions for summary judgment, sending the case to trial. The judge also rejected HCA's attempt to exclude expert witness testimony offered by the AG's office. AG Jackson, who inherited the case from former AG Josh Stein, said HCA 'threw every argument against the wall to try to get this case thrown out' and vowed to keep fighting for emergency and cancer care in WNC.

How to position this
  • HCA failed to kill this case before trial — every legal maneuver was denied, and now a jury of North Carolinians can hear what HCA did to Mission.
  • The court preserved the AG's expert witness, a critical tool for proving HCA's systematic breach of its care commitments.
  • This ruling vindicates years of community advocacy: the legal system is now the last check on HCA's conduct in WNC.
  • Advocates should remind lawmakers and the public that the AG lawsuit is not just symbolic — it can enforce real remedies for the emergency and cancer care HCA has degraded.

On July 28, 2026, North Carolina Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp issued a 55-page order denying all motions for summary judgment and allowing the NC Attorney General's lawsuit against HCA Healthcare over Mission Hospital to proceed to trial. The judge also rejected HCA's effort to bar expert witness testimony called by AG Jeff Jackson's office. The suit alleges HCA failed to maintain the levels of emergency and cancer care it committed to in the 2019 Asset Purchase Agreement. AG Jackson celebrated the ruling and pledged to continue fighting for WNC patients.

Arc — running narrative
2019–2026: HCA buys Mission with binding promises → staffing collapse and four Immediate Jeopardy citations → CON beds awarded amid sanctions → AG lawsuit headed to trial → national coverage of monopoly pricing. Read moreShow less
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
