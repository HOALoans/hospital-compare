WLOS August 13, 2026 CON

Four Hospital Systems — Including HCA's Mission — Compete for 92 More Asheville Beds Ahead of August 19 Hearing

Four hospital systems (Mission/HCA, UNC Health, AdventHealth, and Novant) are making public pitches to NC DHHS for a new license covering 92 acute-care beds in the Asheville area, with the CON public hearing scheduled for August 19. The competition underscores how deeply Mission's safety failures have eroded public trust — three competitors are now seeking to break HCA's grip on western NC care. Mission's separate 95-bed award, still contested, was granted despite four active Immediate Jeopardy sanctions.

How to position this
  • HCA is seeking yet more beds while still under active federal scrutiny — Mission has not resolved the pattern of noncompliance that triggered four Immediate Jeopardy citations since 2019.
  • Three competing systems are racing to fill the care vacuum HCA has created in western NC through staffing cuts and safety failures.
  • NC DHHS awarded Mission 95 beds in April 2026 even as it faced unresolved CMS sanctions — now it wants 92 more; the public hearing is August 19.
  • Advocates should attend the August 19 hearing and demand that any new bed awards be conditioned on Mission meeting federal staffing and safety standards.

Four hospital groups — Mission/HCA, UNC Health, AdventHealth, and Novant — are vying for state approval for 92 new acute-care beds in western North Carolina, with a public hearing set for August 19, 2026 at AB-Tech in Asheville. Mission's proposal would cost approximately $66.8 million and be completed by January 2032. The surge of competitors reflects both regional population need and widespread concern over the quality of care under HCA's stewardship. Mission already holds a contested 95-bed CON award granted in April 2026 despite ongoing federal safety sanctions.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

National Investigation: Mission Hospital Charges $40K for Knee Replacement That Costs $16K Elsewhere in NC — HCA's 'Prepackaged Monopoly' Exposed

A major Washington Post/KFF Health News investigation found that under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement at Mission costs roughly $40,000 versus about $16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center — a price gap traced directly to HCA's monopoly over western NC. RAND data puts Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare, far above the NC benchmark of 280%. The investigation features Asheville employer Katie Button, patient Marcelle Crago, Yale economist Zack Cooper, and Sen. Julie Mayfield of Reclaim Healthcare WNC.

How to position this
  • Mission charges more than twice the NC benchmark for common procedures — a direct result of HCA purchasing a prepackaged monopoly in 2019.
  • Asheville families and employers pay premium prices for 2-star HCAHPS care; the monopoly extracts wealth from WNC while HCA posts record profits.
  • RAND data confirms Mission's prices are 334% of Medicare — 54 points above the NC statewide average — with no offsetting quality advantage.
  • National media is now connecting Mission's pricing power to HCA's acquisition strategy; this story strengthens the case for the AG lawsuit and federal antitrust scrutiny.

Investigative reporter Peter Whoriskey (Washington Post/KFF Health News) documented how Mission Hospital, operating as a regional monopoly since a 1998 merger, charges dramatically higher prices than comparable NC hospitals: knee replacements ~$40,000 vs. ~$16,000 at Catawba Valley; breast biopsies $7,500 vs. $1,700; hernia repairs $17,700 vs. $9,600. RAND 2024 data pegs Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare vs. the NC benchmark of 280%. The piece traces the monopoly from Mission's 1998 merger through the 2015 repeal of state profit limits to HCA's 2019 purchase of what the story calls a 'prepackaged monopoly.' It features Reclaim Healthcare WNC and Sen. Julie Mayfield as accountability voices.

National Nurses United / Too Big to Care Reference Case study

NNU Case Study Maps HCA's National Playbook — Asheville at 58% Market Share, Mission as Cautionary Tale

National Nurses United's Too Big to Care project documents HCA as the 'vanguard of the anticompetitive model': dominate markets, raise prices, cut staffing and services, then extract cash through aggressive billing. The case study cites HCA controlling 58% of Asheville hospital discharges, Mission service cuts and surprise bills after the 2019 acquisition, bedside collections (including a $12,000 demand at a sick patient's bedside), trauma activation fees up to $50,000, and Mission nurses' 2020 union victory — the largest at a non-union Southern hospital since 1975. It also traces Asheville residents' class-action antitrust suits and HCA's decades-long fraud settlements.

How to position this
  • Mission is not an isolated failure — NNU documents the same four-step playbook (dominate, raise prices, cut services, aggressive billing) in communities nationwide.
  • 58% market share in Asheville quantifies the monopoly WaPo/KFF pricing data exposed; advocates can pair this case study with local price comparisons at hearings and in media.
  • Bedside billing and trauma-fee tactics at Mission are part of a documented corporate pattern, not one-off mistakes — useful for AG and consumer-protection arguments.
  • NNU's resource connects WNC's fight to a national nurses' campaign on hospital consolidation; cite it when briefing legislators or coalition partners outside Asheville.

The National Nurses United Too Big to Care case study 'HCA: The Vanguard of the Anticompetitive Model' describes how HCA uses mergers and acquisitions to dominate wealthy Sun Belt markets, then charges more and delivers less. HCA controls at least half the market in seven U.S. markets, including 58% of discharges in Asheville. The Asheville section details Mission Health since HCA's 2019 purchase: clinic closures, reduced charity care, 15 trauma nurses leaving in 2020 alone, at least 79 doctors departing, nurses unionizing in September 2020 despite a $400/hour anti-union campaign, and multiple antitrust lawsuits filed by residents. The study also catalogs national billing abuses (trauma activation fees, point-of-service collections, Envision/TeamHealth ER staffing) and HCA's history of Medicare fraud settlements totaling billions.

WLOS August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent Monitor: HCA Cannot Sell Mission Without Advisory Board Consent; Potential Noncompliance Findings Stand

Members of the independent monitor took public questions on August 4 regarding HCA's 2025 compliance report for Mission Hospital. Monitor representative Rachel Ryan confirmed that under the Asset Purchase Agreement HCA is prohibited from selling Mission during the first 10 years without advisory board consent, and said she is unaware of any HCA desire to sell. The monitor restated findings of potential noncompliance tied to service discontinuation and Mission's pattern of Immediate Jeopardy designations.

How to position this
  • The independent monitor has found potential noncompliance for a third straight year — HCA has not corrected the service degradation and safety failures identified since the 2019 acquisition.
  • The APA's no-sale clause without advisory board consent gives the community a legal lever — but only if the monitor and AG actively enforce it.
  • Repeated Immediate Jeopardy citations are themselves potential noncompliance triggers under the APA; HCA has had four since 2021 and submitted no staffing plans to the federal monitor as of July 2026.
  • Community Q&A turnout of 90+ shows growing WNC public demand for accountability — advocates should sustain pressure through the CON hearing process and AG lawsuit.

WLOS reported on the August 4 public Q&A hosted by Dogwood Health Trust and the independent monitor, covering HCA's 2025 compliance obligations at Mission Hospital. Monitor rep Rachel Ryan clarified that the APA bars HCA from selling Mission within the first 10 years without advisory board approval, and that she is unaware of any HCA intent to sell. Ryan also restated 2025 report findings of potential noncompliance relating to discontinued services and repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations — the same issues at the heart of the NC AG lawsuit. Over 90 community members attended the August 4 webinar.

NC Department of Justice / BPR July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA Summary Judgment — NC Attorney General's Mission Hospital Lawsuit Proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment and ruled that the 2019 Asset Purchase Agreement requires HCA to actually provide — not merely preserve — emergency and cancer care services at Mission. AG Jeff Jackson called the ruling a key victory; the lawsuit originally filed in 2023 by then-AG Josh Stein now advances toward trial. The judge also found HCA's narrower interpretation of the APA — that only total elimination of services would constitute a breach — was rejected.

How to position this
  • HCA's attempt to avoid accountability was rejected by a judge — the court confirmed that degrading services, not just eliminating them, can breach the APA.
  • The AG lawsuit surviving summary judgment is a major inflection point: HCA must now face the full weight of evidence on service cuts, staffing collapse, and Immediate Jeopardy citations at trial.
  • The ruling aligns with the independent monitor's concurrent finding of potential noncompliance — both legal and contractual accountability tracks are advancing simultaneously.
  • Advocates should amplify AG Jackson's stated commitment to see this lawsuit through; the case is Asheville's most direct legal tool for forcing HCA to honor its 2019 commitments.

On July 28, 2026, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Earp denied HCA Healthcare's motion for summary judgment in the state AG's lawsuit over Mission Hospital service obligations. The judge ruled that the APA requires HCA to take steps necessary to actually provide emergency and cancer services, rejecting HCA's argument that only complete service elimination would be a breach. AG Jeff Jackson declared the ruling a key victory, and the lawsuit — originally filed by then-AG Josh Stein in 2023 — now proceeds. The ruling coincided with the independent monitor's report of potential noncompliance and a federal finding that Mission had not achieved substantial compliance with its CMS Enhanced Plan of Correction.

HCA Q2 2026: $20.2B Revenue, $7.62 EPS, $2.1B Buyback — But Full-Year EPS Guidance Cut on Payer Mix Shift

HCA Healthcare reported Q2 2026 revenue of $20.23 billion (+8.7% year-over-year) and diluted EPS of $7.62, while repurchasing $2.1 billion in shares during the quarter.   • HCA's $20.2B quarterly revenue makes its failure to submit staffing plans at Mission — as flagged by the federal monitor — a choice, not a constraint.
  • The EPS guidance cut was caused by payer mix shift, not by Mission investment — HCA is cutting earnings forecasts while continuing to extract capital from a federally sanctioned hospital.
  • Investors are absorbing a guidance cut while HCA simultaneously faces an AG lawsuit and a federal noncompliance finding at Mission; the company discloses none of these liabilities as material risks in IR communications.

HCA Healthcare's Q2 2026 results showed revenue of $20.23 billion (+8.7% YoY), net income of $1.699 billion, and diluted EPS of $7.62. The company repurchased $2.1 billion in shares during the quarter and had $7.2 billion remaining under its share repurchase authorization.
