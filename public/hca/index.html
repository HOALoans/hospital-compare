Asheville Watchdog / NC Health News August 4, 2026 Monitor

Dogwood & Independent Monitor Hold Public Webinar on 2025 HCA Compliance Report — Third Straight Year of Potential Noncompliance

Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors hosted a community Q&A webinar on August 4 to discuss the 2025 HCA Mission monitoring report, which cited two areas of potential noncompliance for the third consecutive year. The monitor found that HCA had discontinued a rehabilitation program and that Mission's repeated federal safety sanctions constituted potential violations of the 2019 Asset Purchase Agreement. The findings have been referred to the NC Attorney General's Office.

How to position this
  • Third consecutive year of potential noncompliance — HCA has never achieved a clean compliance report under the 2019 sale agreement.
  • Monitor documented that a CarePartners rehabilitation program dropped to zero caseload by April 2025 — a functional service elimination HCA did not disclose proactively.
  • Repeated federal safety sanctions (4 Immediate Jeopardy citations) are themselves cited as APA violations, compounding the legal and regulatory exposure.
  • Community turnout of 90+ at the August 4 webinar signals sustained public pressure — advocates should amplify findings before the AG's 30-day review window closes.

Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors Inc. held a public webinar on August 4, 2026, to explain findings from their 2025 annual monitoring report — the third straight year the monitor has flagged potential noncompliance with the terms of HCA's 2019 purchase of Mission Health. The report identified two areas of concern: Mission's repeated federal safety sanctions under an Enhanced Plan of Correction, and the functional discontinuation of an industrial rehabilitation program at CarePartners. Dogwood sent a formal letter to the NC Attorney General's Office on July 22 triggering a 30-day review period. More than 90 community members attended the webinar.

NC DOJ / WLOS / Asheville Watchdog July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA Summary Judgment — AG Jackson's Lawsuit Over Mission Staffing and Emergency Care Advances

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, ruling that the 2019 purchase agreement requires HCA to 'take all steps necessary to actually provide' emergency and cancer services at Mission Hospital. The court specifically preserved claims that HCA failed emergency and trauma obligations through unsafe nurse-to-patient ratios and understaffing. AG Jeff Jackson called it a decisive rejection of HCA's attempt to avoid trial.

How to position this
  • The court ruled HCA must 'take all steps necessary to actually provide' emergency and cancer services — directly rebutting HCA's contractual minimalism defense.
  • Understaffing and unsafe nurse-to-patient ratio claims will go to trial — the legal record will now expose Mission staffing collapse (6.1 to 3.7 FTE/bed) under oath.
  • HCA's failed summary judgment bid cost time and legal fees while Mission patients continued to face a 2-star, understaffed hospital — accountability delayed is accountability denied.
  • Advocates: the lawsuit now advances with all core claims intact — sustained public, media, and congressional pressure is essential to maintain momentum through trial.

On July 28, 2026, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp largely denied HCA Healthcare's motion for summary judgment in the NC Attorney General's lawsuit over Mission Hospital. The court agreed that the Asset Purchase Agreement requires HCA to actually provide emergency and cancer care — not merely maintain structures — and allowed the understaffing and unsafe nurse-to-patient ratio claims to proceed. The court also denied HCA's motion to exclude the AG's expert witness on rural healthcare access. AG Jackson said HCA 'threw every argument against the wall' and none of them stuck.

Asheville Watchdog / Dogwood Health Trust July 24, 2026 Noncompliance

Independent Monitor Flags Two Noncompliance Areas; Mission Races July 26 Medicare/Medicaid Deadline

The 2025 HCA Mission monitoring report, released July 24, cited potential noncompliance with the Asset Purchase Agreement across two areas: repeated federal safety sanctions placing Medicare/Medicaid funding at risk, and the discontinuation and degradation of services including rehabilitation and oncology. Mission faced a hard CMS deadline of July 26 to demonstrate substantial compliance with its Enhanced Plan of Correction or face potential Medicare and Medicaid termination.

How to position this
  • Mission's Medicare/Medicaid funding was at stake through July 26 — HCA's failure to achieve CMS compliance created an existential financial risk for WNC's only Level 2 trauma center.
  • The monitor's finding of degraded oncology and emergency services directly corroborates the AG's lawsuit allegations — the 2025 report is now evidence in active litigation.
  • Service degradation was not proactively disclosed by HCA — the monitor had to independently identify a zero-caseload rehab program.
  • This is the third consecutive annual monitoring report to flag potential noncompliance. HCA has had seven years since acquisition and still cannot meet baseline APA commitments.

Dogwood Health Trust and Affiliated Monitors released their 2025 HCA monitoring report on July 24, 2026, identifying two areas of potential noncompliance: Mission's ongoing failure to satisfy CMS's Enhanced Plan of Correction (with a July 26 termination deadline) and the functional elimination of the industrial rehabilitation program at CarePartners. The monitor noted it does not minimize challenges but found HCA had degraded services in the emergency and oncology departments. Dogwood formally notified the NC Attorney General's Office, triggering a statutory review period.

  • Hazen's Helene/WNC framing portrays HCA as a community partner; advocates should counter with the monitor's finding that Mission has degraded emergency and oncology services in the very region HCA highlighted.
  • Congressional oversight should require HCA to address Mission's CMS compliance status, staffing ratios, and AG litigation in any future health system hearing testimony.

On April 28, 2026, HCA CEO Sam Hazen appeared before the House Ways and Means Committee alongside other major health system CEOs. His written testimony focused on affordability, uncompensated care, workforce training, the Helene disaster response in western NC, and eliminating certificate-of-need laws nationally. The testimony did not address Mission Hospital's Immediate Jeopardy record, the federal monitor's repeated noncompliance findings, the staffing crisis, or the active NC AG lawsuit. Becker's Hospital Review / Yahoo Finance July 24, 2026 Margins

HCA Q2 Adjusted EBITDA Margin Holds at 19.9% — Profit Machine Runs as Mission Compliance Costs Mount

HCA's Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA margin of approximately 19.9% on $20.23 billion in revenue reflects the sustained profitability of HCA's national platform. While overall cash flows from operations declined year-over-year to $2.335 billion (from $4.210 billion in Q2 2025), HCA's underlying operating margins remained robust. Advocates note that Mission Hospital, generating revenue within this system, operates with staffing ratios far below NC averages while HCA's system-level margins remain among the highest in the hospital sector.

How to position this
  • A ~20% EBITDA margin is not achieved without cost discipline — Mission's staffing collapse from 6.1 to 3.7 FTE/bed is a feature of HCA's margin model, not an operational accident.
  • HCA's national margin strength is the financial context for Mission's chronic understaffing: lean staffing ratios are a system-wide cost lever, not a WNC-specific anomaly.
  • Advocates should ask: what portion of HCA's $4B quarterly EBITDA is attributable to understaffed Mission operations, and what would margins look like if staffing met the NC average of 5.1 FTE/bed?
  • Despite margin pressure from surgery volume softness, HCA still returned $2B+ to shareholders in Q2 — demonstrating that Mission staffing investment is a choice HCA has consistently declined to make.

HCA Healthcare's Q2 2026 adjusted EBITDA of $4.027 billion represented a margin of approximately 19.9% on $20.23 billion in revenue. Operating cash flows were $2.335 billion for the quarter, down from $4.210 billion in Q2 2025, partly reflecting timing differences. Inpatient and outpatient surgery volumes declined slightly while admissions and ER visits grew. HCA's overall margin performance remains among the strongest of the large publicly traded hospital chains, sustained in part by facilities like Mission operating with lean staffing.
