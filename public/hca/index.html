NC Department of Justice July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA's Motion to Dismiss — AG Jackson's Lawsuit Proceeds

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp denied HCA's motion for summary judgment on July 28, ruling that all claims in AG Jeff Jackson's lawsuit will proceed. The court agreed that HCA's purchase agreement requires it to actually provide — not merely make available — emergency and cancer services at Mission. AG Jackson said HCA 'threw every argument against the wall' to get the case dismissed.

North Carolina Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp issued a 55-page ruling on July 28, 2026, largely denying HCA's motion for summary judgment and allowing all claims in AG Jeff Jackson's lawsuit to proceed. The court agreed with the AG that HCA's Asset Purchase Agreement obligates it to actually provide emergency and cancer services at Mission Hospital — not merely maintain infrastructure. The judge found one narrow issue — whether Mission was required to embed an ENT surgeon in the emergency department as part of Level 2 trauma obligations — in HCA's favor. The ruling is a significant legal defeat for HCA and validates years of advocacy by community organizations and former Mission staff.

  • Court rules HCA must actually provide — not merely offer — emergency and cancer services under its 2019 purchase contract
  • AG Jackson's full case proceeds; HCA could not escape accountability on summary judgment
  • Ruling validates community complaints that HCA has systematically reduced services since acquiring Mission in 2019
  • Lawsuit focuses on the same commitments HCA made when acquiring a nonprofit hospital — commitments it has repeatedly failed to honor

The independent monitor overseeing HCA's compliance with Mission Hospital's 2019 sale agreement formally notified the NC Attorney General's office of two areas of 'potential noncompliance' as a critical July 26 federal deadline arrived. Mission was required by that date to demonstrate substantial compliance with its Enhanced Plan of Correction or face termination from Medicare and Medicaid. The monitor's findings — covering discontinued and degraded services and repeated threats to patient safety — arrive as the AG's lawsuit reaches a pivotal legal stage.

The independent monitor responsible for overseeing HCA's 2019 Asset Purchase Agreement with Mission Health found two areas of potential noncompliance and formally referred them to the NC Attorney General's office in July 2026. The findings included the continued diminishment of services and repeated Immediate Jeopardy findings that the monitor said signaled threats to patient safety inconsistent with HCA's acquisition commitments. Mission simultaneously faced a July 26 federal deadline to show CMS it had achieved substantial compliance with its Enhanced Plan of Correction or risk Medicare and Medicaid termination. The convergence of the monitor's referral and the federal compliance deadline represents the most serious regulatory pressure Mission has faced since the 2019 acquisition.

  • Independent monitor formally referred HCA to the AG for potential violations of its own purchase commitments — not just regulatory infractions
  • Federal deadline (July 26) for Mission to demonstrate Medicare/Medicaid compliance arrived at the same moment as the monitor's noncompliance finding

Even as Mission Hospital won conditional state approval to add 95 acute-care beds — expanding patient capacity by nearly 13% by 2031 — its own CON application projects staff growth of only 3.7% over seven years. Carolina Public Press / NC Health News April 4, 2026 Safety

Mission Hospital's CMS 'Merry-Go-Round': Four Immediate Jeopardy Citations in Five Years, Still No Systemic Fix

With four Immediate Jeopardy citations since HCA's 2019 acquisition, Mission Hospital is trapped in a cycle of short-term fixes and recurring federal violations, according to reporting by Carolina Public Press and NC Health News. State Sen. Julie Mayfield called for structural regulatory reform, warning that the enforcement cycle enables HCA to treat each citation as a standalone event rather than evidence of systemic failure. Mission's nursing union chair said staff continue to flag unsafe nurse-to-patient ratios as the root cause.

Carolina Public Press and NC Health News documented how Mission Hospital's four Immediate Jeopardy citations in five years — the most serious federal safety finding — reflect a recurring pattern of noncompliance rather than isolated incidents. State Sen. Julie Mayfield, representing Buncombe County, called out the enforcement 'merry-go-round' in which HCA repeatedly submits plans of correction, receives temporary relief, and then triggers new violations. Mission's nursing union leaders said systemic understaffing — not individual errors — is the driver, and that each citation has ended with what one union leader called 'bandaid solutions.' Legislators and advocates are pushing for regulatory changes that would give CMS more tools to compel structural reform rather than paper compliance.

  • Four IJ citations in five years is a pattern, not a series of accidents — CMS's own January 2026 rejection letter cited 'systemic and recurring patterns of harm'
  • Union nurses identify chronic understaffing — not training failures — as the root cause of repeat citations
  • Regulatory 'merry-go-round' lets HCA avoid accountability by treating each citation as discrete; advocates want structural enforcement reform
  • Mission is the only NC hospital with a nurses' union — formed in 2020, one year after HCA's acquisition — a direct response to deteriorating conditions

HCA Healthcare reported Q2 2026 revenue of $20.23 billion, up 8.7% year-over-year, with net income of $1.699 billion and diluted EPS of $7.62. Despite the top-line beat, HCA cut its full-year net income guidance to $6.3–$6.7 billion from a prior range of $6.5–$7.0 billion, citing a $400 million payer-mix headwind as ACA exchange enrollees lost coverage and converted nearly one-for-one to uninsured patients. Adjusted EBITDA guidance was also trimmed to $15.4–$16.1 billion.

HCA Healthcare announced Q2 2026 results on July 24, reporting $20.23 billion in revenue — an 8.7% increase year-over-year — and diluted EPS of $7.62, topping analyst estimates. Net income of $1.699 billion was up modestly from $1.653 billion in Q2 2025. However, HCA revised its full-year 2026 guidance downward, narrowing net income guidance to $6.3–$6.7 billion and EBITDA guidance to $15.4–$16.1 billion, driven by a $1.0–$1.2 billion expected exchange-coverage headwind for the full year. Free cash flow dropped sharply in Q2, raising questions about the sustainability of the company's aggressive shareholder-return programs.

  • Even with guidance cuts, HCA expects $6.3–$6.7B in net income in 2026 — while Mission Hospital cannot fund adequate staffing to meet federal safety standards

HCA Previews Q2 Early — Revises 2026 Revenue Guidance to $77–$79.5B Amid ACA Coverage Losses

Ten days before its official earnings call, HCA released preliminary Q2 results and revised its 2026 annual revenue guidance to $77.0–$79.5 billion, narrowed from $76.5–$80.0 billion. The early disclosure flagged a $400 million payer-mix headwind from ACA exchange coverage losses, with uninsured conversions running higher than originally modeled. The company also noted a roughly equal $400 million offset from Medicaid Supplemental Payment Programs, primarily tied to Florida state-directed payments.

HCA filed an 8-K on July 14, 2026, pre-announcing Q2 results and revising full-year 2026 guidance. Revenue was preliminarily set at approximately $20.23 billion for the quarter, and full-year revenue guidance was adjusted to $77.0–$79.5 billion. The revision acknowledged that ACA exchange coverage losses were converting to uninsured at a rate 'closer to one-for-one' — substantially worse than the 80–85% assumption built into original guidance. A $400 million Medicaid supplemental benefit, concentrated in Florida, helped cushion the blow but raised questions about geographic equity in HCA's financial model.

  • HCA's financial complexity — hedging exchange losses with Florida Medicaid windfalls — illustrates how profits are extracted nationally while local hospitals like Mission face unresolved staffing crises
  • Early guidance cut signals HCA's financial model is under strain, yet no staffing reinvestment for Mission has been announced
  • ACA coverage losses disproportionately affect safety-net patients — the same population Mission serves in WNC — further pressuring an already-understaffed hospital
  • Guidance revision filed with the SEC but no parallel disclosure to WNC community about what coverage losses mean for Mission capacity

The company had lost $150 million in Q1 and $400 million in Q2 from payer-mix shifts as exchange enrollees lost coverage. Despite the margin pressure, HCA maintained its buyback program and did not announce any reallocation of capital toward understaffed facilities.

Healthcare Dive coverage of HCA's July 2026 guidance revision detailed how the company's exposure to ACA exchange coverage losses nearly doubled from its original forecast, now totaling an estimated $1.0–$1.2 billion drag on 2026 results. CFO Mike Marks told investors that uninsured conversion rates from exchange enrollees were running 'closer to one-for-one' rather than the 80–85% originally modeled. The earnings cut came as HCA simultaneously maintained its $10 billion buyback program, signaling continued prioritization of shareholder returns over absorbing the safety-net cost of uninsured patients. For Mission Hospital, which serves WNC's most rural and uninsured communities, the dynamic underscores the structural tension between HCA's investor obligations and local care commitments.

Arc — running narrative
2019–2026: HCA buys Mission with binding promises → staffing collapse and four Immediate Jeopardy citations → CON beds awarded amid sanctions → AG lawsuit headed to trial. Read moreShow less
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
Hazen testimony
HCA CEO Sam Hazen testifies before House Ways and Means. Written testimony pushes eliminating CON laws and highlights Helene response in western NC — without addressing Mission’s Immediate Jeopardy citations, staffing collapse, or the AG lawsuit.
Jul 24, 2026
Q2 Earnings
HCA reports strong Q2 2026 results. Mission's noncompliance is not mentioned on the earnings call. "Operational efficiency" language contrasts sharply with the concurrent federal monitor finding of zero staffing plans submitted by Mission.
Jul 26, 2026
Deadline passes
The Enhanced Plan of Correction deadline expires. The federal monitor confirms Mission has not achieved substantial compliance and has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the same deficiencies driving every prior sanction.
Jul 28, 2026
Trial confirmed
Judge denies HCA's summary judgment motion. The NC Attorney General's lawsuit will go to trial. A court will now assess whether HCA honored its legal commitments to WNC. Reclaim's years of documentation, testimony, and public pressure have contributed directly to this moment.
