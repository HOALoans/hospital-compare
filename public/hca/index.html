WLOS August 5, 2026 Monitor

Independent Monitor: HCA Cannot Sell Mission Without Advisory Board Consent — Sale Restrictions Run 10 Years

Following the August 4 community Q&A webinar, WLOS reported that monitor representative Rachel Ryan clarified HCA is contractually barred from selling Mission Hospital without advisory board consent during the first 10 years of ownership. Ryan also restated the 2025 report's findings of potential noncompliance on service discontinuation and repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations. The public webinar drew 90-plus attendees with questions about HCA's future ownership intentions.

How to position this
  • Even as community members ask whether a better operator could take over, HCA's APA locks Mission into HCA's hands for a decade without advisory board approval — trapping WNC patients under a system with 4 Immediate Jeopardy citations.
  • This is the third consecutive year the independent monitor has flagged HCA for potential noncompliance — a pattern, not an isolated incident.
  • The monitor's public Q&A format underscores how little routine transparency HCA provides: the community must organize formal webinars just to get basic compliance information.
  • Service discontinuation and IJ citations are intertwined: both reflect HCA's pattern of degrading care quality in WNC while reporting record national profits.

On August 5, WLOS reported on the independent monitor's public Q&A held the previous day. Monitor rep Rachel Ryan confirmed that under the Asset Purchase Agreement, HCA cannot sell Mission Hospital during the first 10 years without advisory board consent, and said she was unaware of any HCA desire to sell. Ryan reiterated the 2025 monitoring report's findings of potential noncompliance related to discontinued services and multiple Immediate Jeopardy designations — now the third straight year of potential noncompliance findings.

NC DOJ / BPR / WHQR July 28, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies HCA's Motion to Avoid Trial — AG Jackson's Mission Hospital Lawsuit Moves Forward

NC Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp issued a 55-page ruling on July 28, denying HCA's motion for summary judgment and ruling that the AG's core arguments — including that HCA must take all steps necessary to actually provide emergency and cancer services — will go to trial. The court agreed with AG Jeff Jackson that HCA's purchase agreement requires meaningful delivery of services, not just their nominal continuation. HCA had argued only a complete elimination of services would constitute a breach.

How to position this
  • The court's rejection of HCA's narrow 'only total elimination counts as a breach' argument is a direct win for WNC patients — degraded care is legally contestable.
  • AG Jackson's framing — that HCA must actually provide adequate emergency and cancer services, not just keep them nominally open — aligns with everything Mission patients have experienced since 2019.
  • With the lawsuit proceeding, HCA now faces courtroom scrutiny of the same service degradation the independent monitor has flagged for three consecutive years.
  • Ask HCA why its legal team spent years fighting to avoid accountability in court rather than investing those resources in staffing up Mission Hospital.

On July 28, 2026, NC Business Court Judge Julianna Theall Earp denied HCA Healthcare's motion for summary judgment, allowing AG Jeff Jackson's lawsuit to proceed. The court sided with the AG that the APA requires HCA to 'take all steps necessary to actually provide' emergency and cancer care — rejecting HCA's narrow reading that only outright elimination of services would be a breach. The judge found both sides offered reasonable interpretations of the contract language, making a trial necessary to resolve the dispute. The AG's office characterized the ruling as a significant win on key legal arguments.

Arc — running narrative
2019–2026: HCA buys Mission with binding promises → staffing collapse and four Immediate Jeopardy citations → CON beds awarded amid sanctions → AG lawsuit headed to trial. Read moreShow less
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
Hazen testimony
HCA CEO Sam Hazen testifies before House Ways and Means. Written testimony pushes eliminating CON laws and highlights Helene response in western NC — without addressing Mission’s Immediate Jeopardy citations, staffing collapse, or the AG lawsuit.
Jul 24, 2026
Q2 Earnings
HCA reports strong Q2 2026 results. Mission's noncompliance is not mentioned on the earnings call. "Operational efficiency" language contrasts sharply with the concurrent federal monitor finding of zero staffing plans submitted by Mission.
Jul 26, 2026
Deadline passes
The Enhanced Plan of Correction deadline expires. The federal monitor confirms Mission has not achieved substantial compliance and has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the same deficiencies driving every prior sanction.
Jul 28, 2026
Trial confirmed
Judge denies HCA's summary judgment motion. The NC Attorney General's lawsuit will go to trial. A court will now assess whether HCA honored its legal commitments to WNC. Reclaim's years of documentation, testimony, and public pressure have contributed directly to this moment.
What's next
Trial date TBD in NC Business Court. CMS must determine next steps following Mission's failure to achieve substantial compliance — options include continued monitoring, Medicare/Medicaid payment suspension, or termination. HCA Q3 earnings (expected October 2026) will again test whether HCA addresses Mission publicly in investor communications.
