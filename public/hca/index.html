Reclaim Healthcare WNC Asks NC-11 Candidates: Will You Advance the HCA Mission Act?

With Rep. Chuck Edwards exiting office after censure, Reclaim Healthcare WNC pressed both NC-11 candidates — Democrat Jamie Ager and Republican Jennifer Balkcom — on whether they will carry forward the Health Care Accountability Mission Act. The bill, referred to House Ways and Means in September, would give CMS power to impose $10,000-per-day penalties on hospitals that fail federal patient safety standards. The candidates' answers will reveal whether Mission Hospital accountability has a federal champion heading into 2027.

How to position this
  • HCA Mission Act would finally give CMS a financial stick — $10,000/day penalties — to break the Immediate Jeopardy revolving door at Mission Hospital.
  • Both NC-11 candidates must go on record: will they hold HCA accountable, or let the only federal Mission accountability bill die in Ways and Means?
  • HCA CEO Sam Hazen testified before Ways and Means in April 2026 urging CON elimination — but said nothing about Mission's Immediate Jeopardy citations, staffing collapse, or the federal monitor's noncompliance finding.
  • Reclaim Healthcare WNC is keeping Mission accountability on the 2026 ballot — voters deserve to know which candidate will fight for patient safety in WNC.

Reclaim Healthcare WNC asked the two candidates for North Carolina's 11th Congressional District — Democrat Jamie Ager and Republican Jennifer Balkcom — whether they would advance the HCA Mission Act introduced by departing Rep. Chuck Edwards. The bill would expand CMS powers to impose daily financial penalties on hospitals like Mission that fail patient-safety standards. Edwards introduced the legislation in September 2026, but it has no co-sponsors and faces an uncertain future as he leaves office under censure. The candidates' responses will determine whether federal legislative pressure on HCA continues.

Asheville Watchdog October 2, 2026 Lawsuit

Private Antitrust Lawsuit Against HCA and Mission Nears Settlement — After Class Certification Was Denied

A court filing reveals that HCA, Mission Hospital, and plaintiffs in the 2021 private antitrust class-action lawsuit have reached an 'agreement in principle' to settle — but terms and the class of plaintiffs remain unresolved. The lawsuit accused HCA of exploiting Mission's regional monopoly to force insurers into anticompetitive contracts. Just days earlier, a judge had denied class certification, a significant setback for the plaintiffs.

How to position this
  • HCA's monopoly pricing power — documented in the August 2026 WaPo/KFF investigation — is at the heart of this lawsuit: knee replacements at Mission cost $40,000 vs. $16,000 at a comparable NC hospital.
  • A settlement before full adjudication risks letting HCA avoid a public court record of its anticompetitive conduct in WNC.
  • The denial of class certification shows how hard it is for individual patients and employers to get their day in court against a Fortune 100 hospital chain.
  • The NC AG's separate lawsuit (proceeding after the July 28 summary judgment denial) remains the strongest remaining legal check on HCA's obligations to WNC.

A court filing in early October 2026 indicates that HCA Healthcare, Mission Hospital, and plaintiffs in a 2021 private antitrust lawsuit have reached an agreement in principle to settle. The suit alleged HCA used Mission's regional monopoly — controlling 58% of Asheville-area discharges — to impose anticompetitive terms on health insurers, resulting in inflated prices and worse services for WNC residents. The proposed settlement comes after a North Carolina judge denied class certification in September 2026, a significant legal setback. Settlement terms and the scope of the plaintiff class remain unresolved as of early October.

Asheville Watchdog / NC Health News September 24, 2026 Lawsuit

Judge Denies Class Certification in Private Antitrust Case Against HCA and Mission

A North Carolina judge denied class certification in the private antitrust lawsuit accusing HCA and Mission of using their regional monopoly to impose anticompetitive contracts on health insurers — a significant blow to the case. The ruling means the lawsuit cannot proceed as a consolidated class action. The setback underscores why the NC Attorney General's separate lawsuit, which survived summary judgment on July 28, remains the most important live legal check on HCA.

How to position this
  • With the private antitrust class action weakened, the NC AG's lawsuit is now the most critical legal lever — and HCA is fighting it at every step.
  • HCA's market dominance (58% of Asheville discharges) made anticompetitive conduct possible; the class certification denial does not erase that documented harm.
  • Patients and employers cannot effectively sue individually against a $60B+ hospital chain — systemic accountability requires government enforcement.
  • Advocates should press: if HCA's conduct was pro-competitive, why is it settling? Settlement terms must be public and meaningful.

In a ruling dated September 2026, a North Carolina judge denied the plaintiffs' motion for class certification in the private antitrust lawsuit against HCA Healthcare and Mission Hospital. Class certification is a threshold test for whether a group of plaintiffs can proceed together in a single lawsuit. The denial does not end the case outright but severely limits its scope and bargaining power, contributing to the subsequent agreement-in-principle to settle. The NC Attorney General's government enforcement lawsuit, which survived HCA's summary judgment motion on July 28, 2026, remains the primary legal vehicle for holding HCA accountable to its acquisition commitments.

Asheville Watchdog September 9, 2026 Congress

Rep. Edwards Introduces 'HCA Mission Act' to Give CMS $10,000-Per-Day Penalty Power Over Noncompliant Hospitals

Rep. Chuck Edwards (R-NC-11) introduced the Health Care Accountability Mission Act of 2026 (H.R. 10311), which would empower CMS to impose $10,000-per-day fines on hospitals that continue violating federal patient safety standards after an Immediate Jeopardy finding. The bill was referred to the House Ways and Means Committee with no co-sponsors. Mission Hospital has received four Immediate Jeopardy citations since HCA's 2019 acquisition — the most of any hospital in the state.

How to position this
  • Four Immediate Jeopardy citations in seven years under HCA — yet no meaningful financial penalty to date. The HCA Mission Act would change that calculus.
  • HCA CEO Hazen's Ways and Means testimony in April pushed CON repeal and touted Helene response while staying silent on Mission's federal noncompliance and the AG lawsuit.
  • The bill's referral to Ways and Means — where Hazen just testified — puts HCA's lobbying record and Mission's safety failures in the same congressional spotlight.
  • With Edwards leaving office, Reclaim Healthcare WNC is rightly demanding that every NC-11 candidate commit to advancing this legislation.

Congressman Chuck Edwards introduced H.R. 10311, the Health Care Accountability Mission Act of 2026, in direct response to Mission Hospital's pattern of repeated Immediate Jeopardy designations under HCA ownership. The bill would authorize CMS to levy daily financial penalties of up to $10,000 on hospitals that fail to correct life-safety deficiencies. The legislation was referred to the House Ways and Means Committee — the same committee before which HCA CEO Sam Hazen testified in April 2026 about affordability and the CON system, without addressing Mission's safety record. The bill currently has no co-sponsors.

KFF Health News / Washington Post August 9, 2026 Monopoly

WaPo/KFF Investigation: Mission Hospital's Monopoly Prices — Knee Replacement $40K vs. $16K at a Comparable NC Hospital

A major Washington Post / KFF Health News investigation by Peter Whoriskey documented how HCA's Mission Hospital — controlling 58% of Asheville-area discharges — charges commercial insurance prices that are 334% of Medicare, far above the NC benchmark of 280%. Under the same Blue Cross plan, a knee replacement at Mission cost ~$40,000 versus ~$16,000 at Catawba Valley Medical Center. The piece features patient Marcelle Crago, employer Katie Button, Yale economist Zack Cooper, and Sen. Julie Mayfield / Reclaim Healthcare WNC.

How to position this
  • Mission's 334%-of-Medicare commercial pricing is not a market outcome — it is the direct result of HCA inheriting and exploiting a regional hospital monopoly built over decades.
  • A knee replacement at Mission costs $40,000; the same procedure is $16,000 at Catawba Valley under the same Blue Cross plan. WNC residents and employers are paying the monopoly premium.
  • HCA's CON bed award for 95 new beds — granted despite active federal safety sanctions — will only deepen this monopoly and its pricing power.
  • As the NC AG lawsuit proceeds and the federal monitor flags noncompliance, this investigation provides the economic case for why accountability is inseparable from affordability in WNC.

A sweeping investigative piece by Peter Whoriskey (Washington Post / KFF Health News) placed Mission Hospital at the center of the national hospital monopoly-pricing crisis. Rand 2024 data shows Mission's commercial prices at 334% of Medicare — well above the NC hospital average of 280% — while a comparable hospital (Catawba Valley) came in at 237%. The article traces how Mission's nonprofit predecessors built a 1998 regional monopoly, state profit-limit repeals in 2015 opened the door, and HCA bought a 'prepackaged monopoly' in 2019. The piece features WNC employers, patients facing surprise bills, and advocates including Reclaim Healthcare WNC calling for regulatory action.

Arc — running narrative
2019–2026: HCA buys Mission with binding promises → staffing collapse and four Immediate Jeopardy citations → CON beds awarded amid sanctions → AG lawsuit headed to trial → national coverage of monopoly pricing. Read moreShow less
2019
Acquisition
HCA Healthcare, a Fortune 100 for-profit hospital corporation, acquires Mission Health for $1.5 billion, promising to maintain staffing levels, services, and investment in WNC. The NC Attorney General's office extracts legally binding commitments as a condition of approval.
2019–2022
The exodus
More than 200 physicians and 600 nurses leave Mission. Staff-to-bed ratios fall from 6.1 FTE to 3.7 FTE — well below the 5.1 NC average. Patient experience scores drop to 2 stars and stay there. Mission receives its first Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2021.
2023–2024
Sanctions escalate
Blue Ridge Regional Hospital (HCA-owned, Spruce Pine) cited for Immediate Jeopardy in 2023. Mission receives a second and third Immediate Jeopardy citation in 2024–2025. CMS issues an Enhanced Plan of Correction. Reclaim Healthcare WNC formalizes as a 501(c)(3) coalition of physicians, nurses, elected officials, and community members.
Jan 2026
Fourth IJ
Mission is placed in Immediate Jeopardy for the fourth time — three times in less than two years. CEO tells employees via internal message. National Nurses United responds publicly, calling conditions unsafe. HCA's earnings language simultaneously describes "cost discipline" and margin improvement.
Apr 2026
CON awarded
NC DHHS awards Mission 95 new hospital beds — despite active federal compliance orders and the lowest patient experience scores of any CON applicant. Reclaim testifies in opposition, presenting HCAHPS data showing Mission at 2 stars vs. Advent Health at 4 stars every year since 2020.
Apr 28, 2026
Hazen testimony
HCA CEO Sam Hazen testifies before House Ways and Means. Written testimony pushes eliminating CON laws and highlights Helene response in western NC — without addressing Mission’s Immediate Jeopardy citations, staffing collapse, or the AG lawsuit.
Jul 24, 2026
Q2 Earnings
HCA reports strong Q2 2026 results. Mission's noncompliance is not mentioned on the earnings call. "Operational efficiency" language contrasts sharply with the concurrent federal monitor finding of zero staffing plans submitted by Mission.
Jul 26, 2026
Deadline passes
The Enhanced Plan of Correction deadline expires. The federal monitor confirms Mission has not achieved substantial compliance and has submitted no plans to add nursing, trauma, or security staff — the same deficiencies driving every prior sanction.
Jul 28, 2026
Trial confirmed
Judge denies HCA's summary judgment motion. The NC Attorney General's lawsuit will go to trial. A court will now assess whether HCA honored its legal commitments to WNC. Reclaim's years of documentation, testimony, and public pressure have contributed directly to this moment.
Aug 9, 2026
National spotlight
Washington Post / KFF Health News documents Mission's monopoly pricing: ~$40K vs. ~$16K for the same Blue Cross knee replacement an hour away, and commercial rates at 334% of Medicare. The investigation features Reclaim Healthcare WNC and frames Asheville as a national warning about hospital consolidation.
